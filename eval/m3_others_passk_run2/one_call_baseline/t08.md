## Asymptotic Properties of Maximum-Likelihood Estimators

### Key Properties

#### 1. Asymptotic Unbiasedness

[00:22:53–00:23:38] The maximum likelihood estimate (MLE) $\theta_{\text{ml}}$ is a random variable since it is a deterministic function of random observations $X_1, X_n$. We can prove that its expectation is asymptotically unbiased: $\lim_{n \to \infty} E[\theta_{\text{ml}}] = \theta$ (true parameter value). This means that as the sample size $n$ increases, $\theta_{\text{ml}}$ converges to the true value $\theta$.

#### 2. Consistency

[00:24:25–00:25:07] As $n \to \infty$, the MLE converges to the true parameter value $\theta$. This property is called **consistency**. The lecture notes that this is a well-established result from statistics courses.

#### 3. Asymptotic Normality

[00:25:07–00:25:41] For large $n$, the MLE has an approximately normal distribution with mean $\theta$ and variance $\frac{1}{n} \mathcal{I}(\theta^*)^{-1}$, where $\mathcal{I}(\theta^*)$ is the Fisher information matrix. This implies that the variance of the MLE decreases as $n$ increases.

#### 4. Efficiency

[00:26:23–00:26:58] The **Cramér-Rao bound** (referred to as 'Raul Karamer bound' in the ASR) states that among all unbiased estimators, the MLE achieves the lower bound on variance: $\text{Var}(\hat{\theta}) \geq \frac{1}{n \mathcal{I}(\theta^*)}$. This makes the MLE **efficient**—it has the smallest possible variance among unbiased estimators.

### Clarifications and Uncertainties

- The ASR mentions 'Raul Karamer bound' but the correct term is **Cramér-Rao bound** (likely a mispronunciation of 'Cramér').
- The phrase 'feature information matrix' appears to be a misstatement for **Fisher information matrix**.
- The lecture states that the MLE has the smallest possible variance among unbiased estimators, which is the definition of efficiency.
- The ASR says 'this means that no other unbiased estimate can have smaller variance than the MLE'—this is consistent with the Cramér-Rao bound.

### Notes on Terminology

- **Asymptotic unbiasedness**: The bias of the MLE tends to zero as $n \to \infty$.
- **Consistency**: The MLE converges in probability to the true parameter as $n \to \infty$.
- **Asymptotic normality**: The MLE is approximately normally distributed for large $n$.
- **Efficiency**: The MLE achieves the Cramér-Rao lower bound on variance.

### Summary

The MLE has four key asymptotic properties:
1. Asymptotic unbiasedness
2. Consistency
3. Asymptotic normality
4. Efficiency (achieving the Cramér-Rao lower bound)

These properties make the MLE a powerful tool in statistical inference.
