## Variance Interpretation of Observing Another Variable

### Key Definitions and Reasoning

- **Unconditional Variance of X** ($\text{Var}(X)$): Total variance of variable $X$ without considering $Y$.
- **Conditional Variance of X given Y** ($\text{Var}(X|Y)$): Variance of $X$ that depends on the value of $Y$.

### Core Result

The unconditional variance of $X$ is always greater than or equal to the conditional variance of $X$ given $Y$:

$$
\text{Var}(X) \geq \mathbb{E}[\text{Var}(X|Y)]
$$

This inequality holds because the conditional variance $\text{Var}(X|Y)$ is a function of $Y$, and averaging it over all values of $Y$ (to get the unconditional variance) results in a value that is less than or equal to the unconditional variance.

### Interpretation

Observing $Y$ when $X$ and $Y$ are interdependent provides information that reduces the variance of $X$. This is a fundamental result with practical utility in statistical inference.
