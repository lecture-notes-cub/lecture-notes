## Sequential Bayesian Combination in Elephant Example

### [00:47:09–00:47:40] Problem Setup
- Blind wisdomers (sensors) assigned task: estimate mass of unknown animal via different senses
- Each wisdomer has unique sensor: e.g., touch, sound, smell
- Prior distribution for animal mass: positive semi-axis (θ > 0), bounded by θ_max = 30 tons (blue whale mass)

### [00:47:40–00:48:13] First Wisdom (Tactile Observation)
- Probabilistic model: P(x | θ) where x = tactile observation, θ = animal mass
- Updates prior to posterior distribution: P(θ | x) ∝ P(x | θ)P(θ)
- Example: Touching elephant's nose → posterior suggests either small animal or very large animal (e.g., 'b model four')

### [00:48:13–00:48:43] Second Wisdom (Acoustic Observation)
- New probabilistic model: P(y | θ) where y = acoustic observation
- Different observation space than first wisdom (x vs. y)
- Alternative: Maximal likelihood estimate (MLE) but wisdomers use Bayesian inference

### [00:48:44–00:49:16] Combining Observations
- **Key step**: Use posterior from first wisdom as new prior for second wisdom
- Update rule: P(θ | x, y) ∝ P(y | θ)P(θ | x)
- Normalization yields new posterior that integrates both observations

### [00:49:16–00:49:51] Sequential Process
- After first observation: P(θ | x)
- After second observation: P(θ | x, y) = P(θ | x) × P(y | θ) normalized
- Repeated application: Combine n observations → posterior becomes sharply concentrated around true mass

### [00:50:27–00:50:56] Harmonization Mechanism
- Wisdomers avoid direct comparison of raw observations
- Instead: Use posterior from previous step as new prior
- This allows combining models with different observation spaces

### [00:51:26–00:52:06] Bayesian Advantage
- Combines multiple probabilistic models into a single posterior
- Builds complex models from simple components (like 'building blocks')
- Result: Sharper posterior distribution as observations accumulate
