# Maximum Likelihood Estimation

## Definition
The maximum likelihood estimator (MLE) is the value of parameter $\theta$ that maximizes the likelihood function:

$$\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \prod_{i=1}^n f(x_i | \theta)$$

where $f(x_i | \theta)$ is the probability density function (PDF) of the observations $x_i$ given $\theta$.

## Why Log Likelihood?

The likelihood function is a product of $n$ terms. Taking the logarithm converts this into a sum:

$$\ell(\theta) = \log \mathcal{L}(\theta) = \sum_{i=1}^n \log f(x_i | \theta)$$

This is advantageous because:
- **Computational ease**: Summation is easier to maximize than products (avoids numerical underflow/overflow)
- **Differentiability**: Log likelihood is differentiable, making optimization straightforward
