# Product Rule (Chain Rule) for Joint Distributions

**Definition**: The joint distribution $P(X, Y)$ can be decomposed as:
$$P(X, Y) = P(Y | X) \cdot P(X)$$

**Alternative Orderings**: By symmetry, the same joint distribution can be written in different orders:
- $P(X, Y) = P(X | Y) \cdot P(Y)$
- $P(X_1, X_2, ..., X_n) = \prod_{i=1}^{n} P(X_i | X_{i+1}, ..., X_n) \cdot P(X_n)$

**Example**: For $n=3$:
$$P(X_1, X_2, X_3) = P(X_1 | X_2, X_3) \cdot P(X_2 | X_3) \cdot P(X_3)$$

**Key Insight**: This decomposition is known as *product row* in the transcript (a typo for *product rule*). It states that any joint distribution can be decomposed into one-dimensional conditional distributions, which is useful for reducing variance and simplifying computations.
