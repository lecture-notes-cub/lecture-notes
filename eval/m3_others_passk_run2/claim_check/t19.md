# Rare Disease Test Calculation Correction

## Key Definitions
- **True Positive Rate (Sensitivity)**: Probability of testing positive given disease presence (P(Positive | Disease))
- **False Positive Rate**: Probability of testing positive given disease absence (P(Positive | No Disease))
- **Disease Prevalence**: Proportion of population with disease
- **Positive Predictive Value (PPV)**: Probability of having disease given positive test (P(Disease | Positive))

## Corrected Calculation

### Initial Mistake (Student Error)
At [01:58:08–01:58:38], the student incorrectly assumed P(Positive | No Disease) = 0.99, but this is actually the **false positive rate** (1 - specificity).

### Correct Approach
The lecture corrects this by defining:
- P(Positive | Disease) = 0.99 (sensitivity)
- P(Negative | No Disease) = 0.99 (specificity)
- Disease prevalence = 1% (10⁻²)

### Final Calculation
The correct PPV is:
```
PPV = [P(Disease) × P(Positive | Disease)] / [P(Disease) × P(Positive | Disease) + P(No Disease) × P(Positive | No Disease)]
```

Substituting values:
- P(Disease) = 0.01
- P(No Disease) = 0.99
- P(Positive | Disease) = 0.99
- P(Positive | No Disease) = 0.01 (1 - specificity)

```
PPV = (0.01 × 0.99) / (0.01 × 0.99 + 0.99 × 0.01) = 0.01 / 0.02 = 0.01
```

### Result
The probability of having disease given a positive test is **1%** (not 99% as initially thought). This is counterintuitive because the test is 99% accurate.

## Why This Happens
At [02:01:50–02:02:22], the lecture explains that the low PPV occurs because the disease is rare (1% prevalence). Even with a highly accurate test, false positives dominate when the disease is rare.

## Critical Insight
For very rare diseases (e.g., 1% prevalence), a positive test result only indicates a 1% chance of disease. This is why mass testing for rare diseases can be misleading.
