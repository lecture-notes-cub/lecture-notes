# Sample Size vs. Parameter Count and Bayesian Updating with No Observations

## Key Concepts

### Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) and Sample Size
- **MLE justification**: In classical statistics, MLE is justified when $n$ (sample size) is not much larger than $d$ (dimension of estimated parameters), i.e., $n \gg d$.
- **Overfitting in ML**: When $n \leq d$, models overfit (train error $\gg$ validation error). When $n \gg d$, models generalize well (train error $\approx$ validation error).

### Bayesian Updating with No Observations
- **Posterior when $n=0$**: When there are no observations ($n=0$), the posterior distribution equals the prior distribution.
- **Bayes' theorem**: $\text{Posterior} = \text{Prior} \times \text{Likelihood} / \text{Evidence}$

## Examples

### Machine Learning Context
- **Deep neural networks (15-20 years ago)**: Small networks trained on large datasets showed no overfitting because $n \gg d$ (MLE optimal).
- **Modern deep neural networks**: Larger networks trained on large datasets now overfit because $n \not\gg d$ (train error $\gg$ validation error).

### Bayesian Framework
- **$n=0$ case**: Prior distribution is the posterior distribution (no observations, so no update).
- **$n=1$ case**: One observation allows updating the prior to a posterior distribution.
