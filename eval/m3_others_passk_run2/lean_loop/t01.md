# Conditional Probability and Independence

## Definition of Conditional Distribution

The conditional distribution of $X$ given $Y$ for continuous random variables is defined as:

$$P(X | Y) = \frac{P(X, Y)}{P(Y)}$$

This ratio represents how the distribution of $X$ changes when we observe $Y$.

## Intuition Behind Conditional Probability

- Observing $Y$ provides information about $X$.
- If $X$ and $Y$ are independent, then $Y$ contains no information about $X$.

## Independence and Conditional Distribution

When $X$ and $Y$ are independent:

$$P(X, Y) = P(X) \cdot P(Y)$$

Thus, $P(X | Y) = \frac{P(X, Y)}{P(Y)} = P(X)$

This means that the conditional distribution of $X$ given $Y$ is just the marginal distribution of $X$.

## Key Insight

- If $X$ and $Y$ are dependent, observing $Y$ changes the distribution of $X$.
