## Conditional Probability and Independence

### [00:03:04–00:03:35] Basic Definition
- **Conditional distribution**: For continuous random variables $X$ and $Y$, the conditional distribution of $X$ given $Y$ is defined as the ratio of the joint distribution to the marginal distribution of $Y$.
- **Key point**: The definition is stated as "conditional equals joint over marginal" (i.e., $P(X|Y) = \frac{P(X,Y)}{P(Y)}$).
- **Uncertainty**: The ASR mentions "does it actually matter?" regarding whether $X$ and $Y$ are continuous. This suggests potential ambiguity about the scope of the definition (continuous vs. discrete).

### [00:03:35–00:04:12] Conceptual Intuition
- **Intuition**: The conditional distribution shows how the distribution of $X$ changes when we observe $Y$.
- **Key insight**: The definition implies that $Y$ provides information about $X$ only if $X$ and $Y$ are dependent.

### [00:04:13–00:05:01] Independence Case
- **Independence**: If $X$ and $Y$ are independent, then $P(X,Y) = P(X)P(Y)$.
- **Result**: $P(X|Y) = \frac{P(X,Y)}{P(Y)} = \frac{P(X)P(Y)}{P(Y)} = P(X)$.
- **Interpretation**: When $X$ and $Y$ are independent, observing $Y$ gives no information about $X$ (i.e., $Y$ contains no information about $X$).

### [00:05:01–00:05:31] Dependent Case
- **Dependent case**: If $X$ and $Y$ are dependent, then observing $Y$ changes the distribution of $X$.
