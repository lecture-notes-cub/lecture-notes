## Study Notes: Sample Size vs. Parameter Count and Bayesian Updating with No Observations

### [00:40:46–00:41:49] Classical vs. Bayesian Inference
- **Classical statistics**: Maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) yields a point estimate.
- **Bayesian framework**: Inference results in a distribution (posterior).
- *Key distinction*: MLE is a point estimate; Bayesian inference provides a full distribution.

### [00:41:49–00:42:46] Sample Size and MLE Validity
- MLE is justified when $n$ (number of observations) is **not much larger than $d$** (dimension of parameters).
- *Note*: The ASR says "$n$ is not much larger than one" but this appears to be a misstatement (likely meant $n$ is not much larger than $d$).
- MLE has **no theoretical guarantees** when $n$ is not much larger than $d$.

### [00:42:47–00:43:19] Practical Implications in Machine Learning
- When training ML models:
  - If $n \approx d$ (observations comparable to parameters), **overfitting** occurs.
  - If $n \gg d$ (observations much larger than parameters), **good generalization** is observed.
- *Example*: Early deep neural networks (small models + large datasets) avoided overfitting because MLE was optimal.

### [00:43:19–00:43:49] Overfitting in Deep Learning
- When $n \ll d$ (observations smaller than parameters):
  - **Training error** and **validation error** diverge (overfitting).
- When $n \gg d$:
  - No gap between training and validation errors (good generalization).
- *Historical context*: Early deep neural networks (15–20 years ago) had small $d$ and large $n$, so they did not overfit.

### [00:44:19–00:44:52] Shift in Deep Learning
- As deep neural networks grew larger ($d$ increased), the condition $n \gg d$ was violated.
- Result: **Overfitting** became prevalent (gap between training/validation errors).

### [00:44:52–00:45:20] Bayesian Framework with No Observations
- Bayesian inference works for **any** $n$ (including $n = 0$).
- When $n = 0$:
  - Posterior distribution = **prior distribution** (no observations to update).
  - *Example*: If prior is uniform, posterior remains uniform.
- *Key insight*: With $n = 1$, posterior updates from prior using the observation.

### [00:45:20–00:45:57] Bayesian Updating with $n = 0$
- **Posterior** = **Prior** when $n = 0$.
- *Why?* No observations → no change to prior knowledge.
- *Example*: If the variable is uniform, posterior is uniform.

### Key Definitions
- **Maximum likelihood estimation (MLE)**: Point estimate from classical statistics.
- **Posterior distribution**: Updated distribution of parameters after observing data (Bayesian).
- **Overfitting**: When model fits training data too closely, causing poor generalization.
- **Parameter count ($d$)**: Number of model parameters.
- **Sample size ($n$)**: Number of observations.

### Summary
- **Classical**: MLE works well when $n \gg d$ but fails otherwise.
- **Bayesian**: Always works, even with $n = 0$ (posterior = prior).
