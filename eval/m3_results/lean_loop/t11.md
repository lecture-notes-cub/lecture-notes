# Frequentist vs Bayesian Interpretation of Randomness

## Key Definitions

- **Frequentist View** (Classical): A random variable is a process whose outcome is *unpredictable by definition*. This represents objective uncertainty.
  - *Timestamp*: [00:34:56–00:35:40]

- **Bayesian View**: A random variable is the result of a *completely deterministic process* whose outcome is unknown due to *subjective ignorance* (lack of information).
  - *Timestamp*: [00:35:40–00:36:16]

## Core Examples

### Tosca Coin Example
- The coin toss outcome is *random* in both views, but the Bayesian view emphasizes that the outcome is *predictable in principle* if we knew all initial conditions.
  - *Timestamp*: [00:36:16–00:36:52]

### Eros Eins Train Arrival
- Arrival time is a random variable with *subjective uncertainty* that changes with new information (e.g., train delay screen).
  - *Timestamp*: [00:37:23–00:39:23]

  - *Key Insight*: Bayesian uncertainty is *updateable* with new information (e.g., delay screen changes the distribution).
  - *Timestamp*: [00:38:54–00:39:23]

## Critical Distinction

- **Frequentist**: Randomness = *objective unpredictability* (inherent to the process).
- **Bayesian**: Randomness = *subjective ignorance* (lack of information about a deterministic process).
