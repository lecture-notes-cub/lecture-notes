# Rare Disease Test Calculation

## Key Definitions
- **True Positive (TP)**: Test positive when disease is present
- **False Positive (FP)**: Test positive when disease is absent
- **Sensitivity**: Probability of testing positive given disease (P(positive | disease))
- **Specificity**: Probability of testing negative given no disease (P(negative | no disease))

### Correct Approach
1. **Disease Prevalence**: Let P(disease) = 0.01 (1% of population has disease)
2. **Test Accuracy**:
   - P(positive | disease) = 0.99 (sensitivity)
   - P(negative | no disease) = 0.99 (specificity) → P(positive | no disease) = 0.01 (false positive rate)
3. **Bayes' Theorem Application**:
   
   $$
   P(disease | positive) = \frac{P(positive | disease) \cdot P(disease)}{P(positive)}
   $$

   Where:
   - $P(positive) = P(positive | disease) \cdot P(disease) + P(positive | no disease) \cdot P(no disease)$
   - $P(no disease) = 0.99$

4. **Calculation**:
   - $P(positive) = (0.99 \cdot 0.01) + (0.01 \cdot 0.99) = 0.0198$
   - $P(disease | positive) = \frac{0.99 \cdot 0.01}{0.0198} = 0.5$ (This is a simplification for the example)

## Why This Happens
- **Rare Disease Effect**: Even with a 99% accurate test, if the disease is very rare (1%), a positive test result only indicates a 1% chance of having the disease.
- **Statistical Implication**: Mass testing for rare diseases can lead to many false positives, making the test less useful for population screening.
