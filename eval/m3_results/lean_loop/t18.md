# Radio Evidence, Low Burglary Posterior, and Model Misspecification

## Key Definitions
- **Radio Evidence**: Information from radio announcements (e.g., earthquake reports) used as evidence in probabilistic models.
- **Low Burglary Posterior**: The computed probability that a thief is present given radio evidence (earthquake announcement), which was 2% in this example.
- **Model Misspecification**: When the probabilistic model used for inference does not accurately represent real-world dependencies (e.g., assuming independence between earthquakes and thieves).

## Example: Bayesian Analysis of Earthquake and Theft

**Scenario**: The speaker computes the posterior probability of a thief being in their house given a radio announcement of an earthquake.

- **Joint Probability Calculation**:
  - Numerator: $P(T=1, E=1, R=1, A=1)$ (thief, earthquake, radio announcement, alarm triggered)
  - Denominator: $\sum_{T, E, R} P(T, E, R, A=1)$
  - Result: ~2% posterior probability of thief given earthquake announcement ([01:25:56–01:26:22])

**Why the Low Posterior?**
- The model assumed earthquakes and thieves were independent, but in reality:
  - Thieves listen to radio and increase activity after small earthquakes (e.g., interpreting seismic activity as less likely to be theft)
  - This dependency was not captured in the model, leading to an overly optimistic posterior

**Consequence**:
- The speaker's model was misspecified (independent assumption), causing the robbery ([01:27:07–01:28:13])

## Critical Insight
- **Model Misspecification Impact**: The model's assumption of independence between earthquakes and thieves led to a 2% posterior probability of theft, but real-world dependency (thieves react to earthquakes) increased the actual risk.
