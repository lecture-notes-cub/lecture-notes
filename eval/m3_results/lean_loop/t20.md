# Gamma-Poisson Bayesian Update

## Final Posterior Distribution

The posterior distribution for $\lambda$ given Poisson observations $X_1, \dots, X_n$ and a Gamma prior $\text{Gamma}(\alpha, \beta)$ is a Gamma distribution with updated parameters:

$$
\lambda | X_1, \dots, X_n \sim \text{Gamma}(\alpha + \sum_{i=1}^n X_i, \, \beta + n)
$$

**Derivation Summary** (from [03:17:17–03:18:48]):

1. **Joint Distribution**: The joint distribution is proportional to $\prod_{i=1}^n \frac{\lambda^{X_i} e^{-\lambda}}{X_i!} \times \frac{\beta^\alpha}{\Gamma(\alpha)} \lambda^{\alpha-1} e^{-\beta \lambda}$.
2. **Posterior Simplification**: After integrating out $\lambda$, the posterior is proportional to $\lambda^{\alpha + \sum X_i - 1} e^{-(\beta + n)\lambda}$.
3. **Result**: This is a Gamma distribution with shape $\alpha + \sum X_i$ and rate $\beta + n$.

## Key Definitions

- **Poisson Distribution**: $P(X_i = x_i | \lambda) = \frac{\lambda^{x_i} e^{-\lambda}}{x_i!}$
- **Gamma Distribution**: $\text{Gamma}(\alpha, \beta) \sim \frac{\beta^\alpha}{\Gamma(\alpha)} \lambda^{\alpha-1} e^{-\beta \lambda}$

## Example

If $X_1, \dots, X_n$ are Poisson observations with mean $\lambda$, and the prior is $\text{Gamma}(\alpha, \beta)$, then the posterior is $\text{Gamma}(\alpha + \sum X_i, \beta + n)$.
