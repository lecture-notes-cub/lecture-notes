# Bayesian Parameter Estimation vs. Point Estimates

## Key Definitions
- **Prior Distribution**: Probability distribution reflecting initial knowledge or beliefs about parameter \(\theta\) before observing data.
- **Posterior Distribution**: Updated probability distribution of \(\theta\) after incorporating observed data \(X_1, X_2, ..., X_n\) via Bayes' theorem.
- **Bayes' Theorem**: \(P(\theta | X) = \frac{P(X | \theta) P(\theta)}{P(X)}\), where \(P(X | \theta)\) is likelihood, \(P(\theta)\) is prior, and \(P(X)\) is evidence.

## Core Reasoning
- **Frequentist Approach**: Uses maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) to find a single point estimate of \(\theta\) that maximizes the likelihood function.
- **Bayesian Approach**: Derives a full posterior distribution over \(\theta\) by combining prior beliefs with observed data.

## Example
- **Likelihood Function**: For independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) observations, the likelihood is the product of individual probabilities: \(P(X_1, ..., X_n | \theta) = \prod_{i=1}^n P(X_i | \theta)\).
- **Posterior Calculation**: Posterior \(P(\theta | X)\) is proportional to likelihood times prior: \(P(\theta | X) \propto P(X | \theta) P(\theta)\), normalized by evidence \(P(X)\).

## Key Difference
- **Point Estimate vs. Distribution**: Frequentist MLE yields a single point estimate; Bayesian inference yields a full distribution of possible values for \(\theta\), capturing uncertainty.
