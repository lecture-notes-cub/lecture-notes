# Product Rule and Chain Rule for Joint Distributions

## Key Definitions

- **Joint Distribution**: Probability distribution of multiple random variables.
- **Product Rule (Chain Rule)**: For any set of random variables $X_1, X_2, ..., X_n$, the joint distribution can be decomposed as:
  
  $$
  p(X_1, X_2, ..., X_n) = \prod_{i=1}^{n} p(X_i | X_1, X_2, ..., X_{i-1})
  $$

- **Alternative Variable Orderings**: The decomposition can be rearranged in different orders (e.g., $p(X_n | X_1, ..., X_{n-1}) \cdot p(X_{n-1} | X_1, ..., X_{n-2}) \cdot ... \cdot p(X_1)$).

## Examples

- **Basic Decomposition** (from [00:06:41–00:07:23]):
  $$
  p(X, Y) = p(X | Y) \cdot p(Y)
  $$

- **Alternative Ordering** (from [00:07:56–00:08:35]):
  $$
  p(X, Y) = p(Y | X) \cdot p(X)
  $$

- **General Case** (from [00:07:23–00:07:56]):
  $$
  p(X_1, X_2, ..., X_n) = p(X_1 | X_2, ..., X_n) \cdot p(X_2 | X_3, ..., X_n) \cdot ... \cdot p(X_n)
  $$

## Important Notes

- The lecture uses the term 'product row' (likely a misstatement for 'product rule') and 'chain row' (likely 'chain rule') to describe this decomposition.
- The decomposition is valid for any order of variables, which is useful for simplifying computations in Bayesian networks and other probabilistic models.
- The lecture emphasizes that this is a fundamental identity for handling interdependent variables.
