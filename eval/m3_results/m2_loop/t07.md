# Maximum-Likelihood Estimation

## Definition
The maximum-likelihood estimator (MLE) is the value of parameter \(\theta\) that maximizes the likelihood function, which is the product of the probability density functions (PDFs) of observed data points.

## Likelihood Function
For independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) observations \(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n\) from a distribution with unknown parameter \(\theta\), the likelihood function is:

$$\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \prod_{i=1}^n f(x_i; \theta)$$

where \(f(x_i; \theta)\) is the PDF of the distribution at observation \(x_i\).

## Why Log Likelihood?
- The likelihood function is a product of functions, which is difficult to maximize directly.
- Taking the logarithm converts the product into a sum: \(\ln \mathcal{L}(\theta) = \sum_{i=1}^n \ln f(x_i; \theta)\).
- Maximizing the log-likelihood is equivalent to maximizing the likelihood (since \(\ln\) is a monotonically increasing function).
- This simplifies computation because sums are easier to handle than products (e.g., numerical stability, analytical solutions).

## Key Insight
The log-likelihood is used because it transforms the product of distributions into a sum, making the optimization problem more tractable while preserving the maximum point.
