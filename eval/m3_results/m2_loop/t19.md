# Rare-Disease Test Calculation

## Key Definitions
- **True Positive Rate (Sensitivity)**: Probability of testing positive given disease presence (P(positive | disease))
- **False Positive Rate (1 - Specificity)**: Probability of testing positive given disease absence (P(positive | no disease))
- **Disease Prevalence**: Proportion of population with disease (P(disease))
- **Positive Predictive Value (PPV)**: Probability of having disease given positive test (P(disease | positive))

## Calculation Example

The lecture calculates the PPV for a rare disease with a 99% accurate test:

1. **Given**:
   - Disease prevalence: 1 in 100 (0.01)
   - Test sensitivity: 99% (P(positive | disease) = 0.99)
   - Test specificity: 99% (P(negative | no disease) = 0.99)

2. **Student Error** (at [01:58:38–01:59:12]):
   - Incorrectly assumed P(positive | no disease) = 0.99
   - Correctly identified: P(negative | no disease) = 0.99 → P(positive | no disease) = 0.01

3. **Correct Calculation** (at [02:00:08–02:01:50]):
   
   $$
   \text{PPV} = \frac{P(\text{disease}) \times \text{sensitivity}}{P(\text{disease}) \times \text{sensitivity} + (1 - P(\text{disease})) \times (1 - \text{specificity})}
   $$

   $$
   = \frac{0.01 \times 0.99}{0.01 \times 0.99 + 0.99 \times 0.01} = \frac{0.0099}{0.0198} = 0.05
   $$

   **Note**: The lecture states the final PPV is 5%, not 1% (a common student error). The 1% figure was a miscalculation where the denominator was incorrectly simplified.

4. **Key Insight** (at [02:02:22–02:02:52]):
   - Even with a 99% accurate test, if the disease is rare (1%), the PPV is only 5%.
   - This highlights why mass testing for rare diseases can be misleading: many positive results are false positives.

## Why This Matters
- **Real-world application**: For rare diseases, a positive test result does not mean high probability of disease.
- **Critical for interpretation**: Always consider disease prevalence when interpreting test results.
