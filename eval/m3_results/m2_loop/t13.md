# Sequential Observation Combination in Bayesian Inference

## Key Example: Elephant Mass Estimation

The lecture uses a scenario where multiple 'wisdoms' (sensors) with different probabilistic models estimate the mass of an unknown animal. Each wisdom provides observations that are combined sequentially via Bayesian inference to refine the estimate.

### Prior Distribution
- **Definition**: A distribution over possible masses of animals on Earth, restricted to positive values (mass ≥ 0) and capped at 30 tons (the mass of the blue whale).
- **Timestamp**: [00:47:40–00:48:13]

### First Wisdom (Tactile Observation)
- **Observation**: Touching the elephant's nose
- **Probabilistic Model**: Likelihood function $P(x | \theta)$, where $x$ is tactile observation and $\theta$ is animal mass
- **Bayesian Update**: Computes posterior distribution $P(\theta | x)$ using prior and likelihood
- **Result**: After touching the elephant's nose, the posterior distribution suggests either a very large animal (e.g., elephant) or a snake-like animal
- **Timestamp**: [00:49:16–00:49:51]

### Second Wisdom (Acoustic Observation)
- **Observation**: Listening to the animal's sounds
- **Probabilistic Model**: Likelihood function $P(y | \theta)$, where $y$ is acoustic observation
- **Bayesian Update**: Uses posterior from first wisdom as new prior, computes updated posterior $P(\theta | x, y)$
- **Result**: Combines tactile and acoustic information to refine the estimate
- **Timestamp**: [00:50:56–00:51:26]

### Sequential Combination Process
- **Process**: Repeatedly apply Bayesian inference using posterior from previous step as new prior
- **Result**: After multiple observations, posterior distribution becomes sharply concentrated around a specific mass
- **Timestamp**: [00:52:43–00:53:21]

### Why This Works
- **Key Advantage**: Bayesian framework allows combining different probabilistic models (e.g., tactile, acoustic) into a single coherent estimate
- **Analogy**: Building complex models from simpler components (like 'walls from building blocks')
- **Timestamp**: [00:52:43–00:53:21]

> **Note**: The lecture explicitly states that the elephant example is used to illustrate sequential observation combination. The tactile observation of the elephant's nose leads to a posterior distribution that suggests either a large animal or a snake-like animal. This example demonstrates how Bayesian inference harmonizes multiple sources of information.
