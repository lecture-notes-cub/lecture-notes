# Bayes' Theorem Study Notes

**Definition**: Bayes' theorem is a fundamental result in probability theory that describes how to update the probability of a hypothesis based on new evidence.

## Key Components

- **Prior**: The probability distribution representing our initial knowledge or beliefs about the variable of interest *before* observing new data. As stated in the transcript: "this reflects our prior information of prior knowledge about possible values of y" ([00:17:14–00:17:43]).

- **Likelihood**: The probability of observing the data given the hypothesis. The transcript mentions "observations that are expressed in terms of this livelihood function" (likely a speech error for "likelihood function").

- **Posterior**: The updated probability distribution after incorporating new evidence. The transcript states: "posterior equals the product of likelihood times prior" ([00:16:01–00:16:45]).

- **Evidence**: The normalization constant that ensures the posterior distribution integrates to 1. The transcript explains: "this normalization constant is called evidence" ([00:16:45–00:17:14]).

## Bayes' Theorem Equation

The theorem can be written as:

$$
\text{Posterior} = \frac{\text{Likelihood} \times \text{Prior}}{\text{Evidence}}
$$

Where:
- **Posterior** = Updated probability distribution after observing data
- **Likelihood** = Probability of data given hypothesis
- **Prior** = Initial probability distribution
- **Evidence** = Normalization constant ensuring the posterior integrates to 1

## Conceptual Explanation

Bayes' theorem shows how we can convert our knowledge about a variable of interest. We start with our prior knowledge about possible values of y. Then, we take into account observations that are expressed in terms of the likelihood function. Applying Bayes' theorem, we transform our prior knowledge to posterior knowledge, updating our understanding of the variable's value ([00:17:14–00:17:43]).

## Physical Interpretation

This equation is a way of solving inverse probabilistic problems. It allows us to update our knowledge about the value of y when we observe some information related to y ([00:17:14–00:17:43]).
