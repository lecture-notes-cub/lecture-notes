# Statistical Marginalization in Probability Theory

**Definition**
- Marginalization: The process of integrating out variables to obtain an unconditional distribution from a joint distribution.

**Key Mathematical Expression**
- For two variables: $P(x) = \int P(x|y)P(y) \, dy$ (from [00:09:08–00:10:00])
- This is equivalent to the expectation of $P(x|y)$ over $y$ (from [00:11:17–00:11:49])

**Physical Interpretation**
- Averaging conditional distributions over all possible values of $y$ to obtain the unconditional distribution on $x$ (from [00:11:17–00:11:49])

**Why This Matters**
- This process is fundamental in Bayesian statistics and machine learning for reducing dimensionality.

**Important Clarification**
- *This lecture discusses statistical marginalization (mathematical process), not social marginalization*. The term 'marginalization' in the transcript refers to the statistical concept, not social exclusion or discrimination. Social marginalization is a separate concept in sociology.
