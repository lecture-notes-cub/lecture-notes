# Predicting Unobserved Variables from Joint Probabilistic Models

## Key Concept

To predict an unobserved variable $X$ given an observed variable $Y$ from a joint probabilistic model (joint distribution over all variables), we compute the conditional distribution $P(X|Y)$.

## Mathematical Formulation

Given a joint distribution $P(X, Y, Z)$ over three variables, and observing $Y$, the conditional distribution $P(X|Y)$ is computed as:

$$P(X|Y) = \frac{P(X, Y)}{P(Y)}$$

Where:
- $P(X, Y)$ is the marginal distribution over $X$ and $Y$ (obtained by integrating out $Z$ from the joint distribution $P(X, Y, Z)$)
- $P(Y)$ is the marginal distribution over $Y$ (obtained by integrating out $X$ and $Z$ from the joint distribution $P(X, Y, Z)$)

## Reasoning Process

1. Start with the definition of conditional probability
2. Express both numerator and denominator using the joint distribution
3. Integrate out the unobserved variable $Z$ to obtain the necessary marginals
4. The result is the conditional distribution $P(X|Y)$

## Example

Suppose we have a joint distribution $P(X, Y, Z)$ where:
- $X$ is the variable we want to predict
- $Y$ is the observed variable
- $Z$ is an unobserved variable

When we observe $Y$, we can compute $P(X|Y)$ by:
- Integrating $Z$ out of the joint distribution to get $P(X, Y)$
- Integrating $Z$ out of the joint distribution to get $P(Y)$
- Dividing the two results to get the conditional distribution

## Important Notes

- A probabilistic model is defined as a joint distribution over all variables in the problem
- With access to the joint distribution, we can make arbitrary predictions by computing conditional distributions
- This approach works in theory but may be computationally intensive for high-dimensional problems
