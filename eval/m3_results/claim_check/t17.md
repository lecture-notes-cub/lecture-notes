# Bayesian Alarm Example: Posterior Calculations

## Initial Posterior (Prior = 0.2)
- **Prior**: $P(t=1) = 0.2$ (burglary probability)
- **Posterior**: $P(t=1 | \text{alarm}) \approx 70\%$ (from calculation: $\frac{2 \times 10^{-4}}{2 \times 10^{-4} + 10^{-3}} \approx 0.666$)
  - **Timestamp**: [01:19:19–01:19:53]

## Effect of Increasing Prior by 10x
- **New Prior**: $P(t=1) = 0.2 \times 10 = 2 \times 10^{-1}$ (10x increase)
- **New Posterior**: $P(t=1 | \text{alarm}) \approx 67\%$ (from calculation: $\frac{2 \times 10^{-3}}{2 \times 10^{-3} + 10^{-3}(1 - 2 \times 10^{-3})} \approx 0.666$)
  - **Timestamp**: [01:22:42–01:23:13]
- **Key Insight**: Posterior increased **4x** (from 70% → 67%) instead of 10x (impossible, as probability can't exceed 1)
  - **Timestamp**: [01:23:13–01:23:46]

## Why Posterior Doesn't Scale Linearly
- **Reasoning**: The posterior depends on both prior and likelihood. Increasing prior by 10x only increases posterior by 4x because the likelihood term dominates the denominator.
  - **Timestamp**: [01:21:37–01:22:06]
