## Bayes' Theorem Equation

The theorem is expressed as:

$$
\text{Posterior} = \frac{\text{Likelihood} \times \text{Prior}}{\text{Evidence}}
$$

This is derived from the conditional probability equation:

$$
\mathbb{P}(Y|X) = \frac{\mathbb{P}(X|Y) \cdot \mathbb{P}(Y)}{\mathbb{P}(X)}
$$

Where:
- $\mathbb{P}(Y|X)$: Posterior
- $\mathbb{P}(X|Y)$: Likelihood
- $\mathbb{P}(Y)$: Prior
- $\mathbb{P}(X)$: Evidence

## Conceptual Explanation

[00:17:14–00:17:43] *'Bayes' theorem shows how we can convert our knowledge about variable of interest. We start from prior distribution that reflects our prior information... Then we take into account observations... And applying Bayesian theorem, we transform our prior knowledge to posterior knowledge. So we update our knowledge about the value of y.'*
