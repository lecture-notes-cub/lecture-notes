# Product Rule (Chain Rule) for Joint Distributions

## Definition
The joint distribution $P(X, Y)$ can be written as:
$$P(X, Y) = P(Y | X) \cdot P(X)$$
By symmetry:
$$P(X, Y) = P(X | Y) \cdot P(Y)$$

## Generalization to $n$ Variables
For $n$ variables, the joint distribution $P(X_1, X_2, \dots, X_n)$ decomposes as:
$$P(X_1, X_2, \dots, X_n) = \prod_{i=1}^{n} P(X_i | X_1, X_2, \dots, X_{i-1})$$

## Alternative Orderings
The lecture shows two decompositions:
1. **Forward ordering** (from $X_1$ to $X_n$):
   $$P(X_1, X_2, \dots, X_n) = P(X_1) \cdot P(X_2 | X_1) \cdot \dots \cdot P(X_n | X_{n-1}, \dots, X_1)$$
2. **Reverse ordering** (from $X_n$ to $X_1$):
   $$P(X_1, X_2, \dots, X_n) = P(X_n) \cdot P(X_{n-1} | X_n) \cdot \dots \cdot P(X_1 | X_2, \dots, X_n)$$

## Key Terms
- **Product rule**: The identity that decomposes joint distributions into conditional distributions (sometimes called the chain rule).
- **Chain rule**: The sequential decomposition of joint distributions.
