# Conditional Probability and Independence

## Definition of Conditional Probability

The conditional distribution of $X$ given $Y$ is defined as:

$$P(X | Y) = \frac{P(X, Y)}{P(Y)}$$

This is derived from the joint distribution $P(X, Y)$ divided by the marginal distribution $P(Y)$.

## Independence

Two random variables $X$ and $Y$ are independent if:

$$P(X, Y) = P(X) \cdot P(Y)$$

When $X$ and $Y$ are independent, the conditional distribution simplifies to:

$$P(X | Y) = P(X)$$

## Intuition

- **Conditional probability** shows how the distribution of $X$ changes when we observe $Y$.
- **Independence** means that observing $Y$ contains no information about $X$.

## Example

If $X$ and $Y$ are independent, then $P(X | Y) = P(X)$. This implies that $Y$ provides no information about $X$.
