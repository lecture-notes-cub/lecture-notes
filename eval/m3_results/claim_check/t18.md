# Bayesian Reasoning Example: Radio Evidence and Model Misspecification

## Context
- The speaker computes the probability of a thief being in their house based on:
  - Alarm system sending SMS ([01:23:46–01:24:29])
  - Radio message announcing an earthquake ([01:23:46–01:24:29])

## Key Calculations
- **Joint probability model**: The speaker uses variables T (thief), E (earthquake), R (radio), A (alarm) ([01:24:33–01:25:19])
- **Posterior probability**: After computing, the probability of a thief being present is **~2%** ([01:25:56–01:26:22])
- **Interpretation**: This low probability (2%) was considered unusual, so the speaker went back to their office ([01:26:23–01:26:51])

## Model Misspecification
- **Assumption**: The speaker's model assumed earthquakes and thieves happen independently ([01:27:07–01:27:44])
- **Reality**: Thieves listen to radio and increase activity when they hear about earthquakes ([01:27:44–01:28:13])
- **Consequence**: The model was too optimistic, leading to the apartment being robbed ([01:28:13–01:28:44])

## Why This Matters
- **Radio evidence**: The radio message is used as evidence for an earthquake, but the speaker doesn't know if the earthquake actually occurred ([01:25:56–01:26:22])
- **Bayesian extension**: This example shows how Bayesian reasoning extends classical Boolean logic ([01:28:44–01:29:28])

## Critical Insight
> "The probability that there is a thief, if there was an earthquake, is larger than the probability of thief if there was no earthquake. Why so? Because thieves are not idiots. They also listen to the radio."
> — [01:27:44–01:28:13]
