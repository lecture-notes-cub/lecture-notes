# Sequential Bayesian Inference: Elephant Example

## Key Setup
- **Problem**: Blind wisdomers estimate mass of unknown animal using different senses (touch, sound, smell) [00:47:09–00:47:40]
- **Prior distribution**: Restricted to positive semi-axis (mass ≥ 0) with θ_max = 30 tons (blue whale mass) [00:47:40–00:48:13]

## First Observation (Tactile)
- **Sensor**: Touches animal (e.g., elephant's nose) [00:49:16–00:49:51]
- **Probabilistic model**: Likelihood function P(x|θ) = probability of observing x given animal mass θ [00:48:44–00:49:16]
- **Bayesian update**: Posterior distribution P(θ|x) = [P(x|θ) × Prior(θ)] / Normalizing constant [00:49:16–00:49:51]
- **Result**: After touching elephant's nose, posterior suggests either a very large animal or snake-like animal (bimodal distribution) [00:49:51–00:50:27]

## Second Observation (Acoustic)
- **Sensor**: Listens to animal sounds [00:50:27–00:50:56]
- **Probabilistic model**: Likelihood function P(y|θ) = probability of observing y given animal mass θ [00:50:27–00:50:56]

## Sequential Combination
- **Process**:
  1. Start with prior distribution [00:47:09–00:47:40]
  2. First wisdomer updates to posterior P(θ|x₁) [00:49:16–00:49:51]
  3. Second wisdomer uses P(θ|x₁) as new prior [00:51:26–00:52:06]
  4. Updates to new posterior P(θ|x₁, x₂) = [P(x₂|θ) × P(θ|x₁)] / Normalizing constant [00:52:06–00:52:43]
- **Result**: After multiple observations, posterior becomes sharply concentrated around true mass [00:52:43–00:53:21]

## Why This Works
- **Bayesian advantage**: Combines multiple probabilistic models (from different sensors) [00:52:43–00:53:21]
- **No need for full knowledge**: Wisdomers only need posterior distributions from previous steps [00:51:26–00:52:06]
- **Example**: After two observations, posterior reflects information from both tactile and acoustic sensors [00:52:06–00:52:43]
