# Bayesian Methods: Large-Data Limit and Practical Advantages

## Large-Data Limit

The lecture explains that in the limit when the number of observations $n$ is much larger than the number of parameters $d$ (i.e., $n \gg d$), the posterior distribution in Bayesian inference converges to a delta function around the maximum likelihood estimate (MLE). This means:

- **Theoretical convergence**: Under the condition $n \gg d$, the Bayesian posterior distribution converges to the MLE. [00:53:55–00:54:26]
- **No contradiction**: This shows that classical statistics (which uses MLE) is a limit case of the Bayesian framework. The two approaches are not contradictory but rather complementary. [00:53:22–00:53:54]

## Key Definitions and Clarifications

- **Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE)**: The method that finds parameters maximizing the likelihood of the observed data. [00:53:55–00:54:26]
- **Posterior Distribution**: The distribution of parameters given the observed data and prior beliefs. [00:53:55–00:54:26]
- **Delta Function**: A mathematical concept representing a point mass at a specific value (here, the MLE). [00:53:55–00:54:26]
