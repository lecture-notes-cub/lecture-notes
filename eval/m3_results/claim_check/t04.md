# Marginalization and Expectation Interpretation

**Definition**: Marginalization refers to the process of obtaining the unconditional distribution of a random variable by integrating (or summing) over all other variables in the joint distribution.

**Key Insight**: The unconditional distribution of $X$ can be derived from conditional distributions $P(X|Y)$ by integrating over $Y$:

$$P(X) = \int P(X|Y) P(Y) \, dY$$

This is equivalent to taking the expectation of $P(X|Y)$ with respect to $Y$.

**Physical Interpretation** (from lecture):
- [00:10:46–00:11:17] Imagine knowing all conditional distributions $P(X|Y)$ for arbitrary $Y$ values.
- [00:10:46–00:11:17] The unconditional distribution $P(X)$ is obtained by averaging these conditional distributions over all $Y$.
- [00:10:46–00:11:17] This is analogous to integrating out $Y$ from the joint distribution $P(X,Y)$.

**Example**: For two random variables $X$ and $Y$:
- [00:09:08–00:10:00] $P(X) = \int P(X|Y) P(Y) \, dY$ (marginalization)
- [00:09:08–00:10:00] This can be interpreted as the expectation of $P(X|Y)$ with respect to $Y$.

**Generalization**:
- [00:11:49–00:12:44] By induction, this process extends to multiple variables. For example, to obtain $P(X_1, X_2, ..., X_k)$ from a joint distribution over $X_1, ..., X_n$, integrate out the remaining variables $X_{k+1}, ..., X_n$.
