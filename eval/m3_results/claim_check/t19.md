# Rare Disease Test Calculation

## Key Definitions
- **True Positive Rate (Sensitivity)**: Probability of testing positive given disease presence (P(positive | disease))
- **False Positive Rate (1 - Specificity)**: Probability of testing positive given disease absence (P(positive | no disease))
- **Disease Prevalence**: Proportion of population with disease (P(disease))
- **Positive Predictive Value (PPV)**: Probability of having disease given positive test (P(disease | positive))

## Corrected Calculation

### Step 1: Identify the error
The student initially used an incorrect formula: `P(positive | disease) = 0.99` but forgot to account for the negative test accuracy.

### Step 2: Corrected formula
The correct calculation for PPV is:

```
PPV = [P(disease) * P(positive | disease)] / [P(disease) * P(positive | disease) + (1 - P(disease)) * P(positive | no disease)]
```

### Step 3: Apply values
Given:
- Disease prevalence = 1% (0.01)
- Test sensitivity = 99% (P(positive | disease) = 0.99)
- Test specificity = 99% (P(negative | no disease) = 0.99 → P(positive | no disease) = 0.01)

Calculation:

```
PPV = [0.01 * 0.99] / [0.01 * 0.99 + 0.99 * 0.01] = 0.0099 / 0.0198 = 0.01 (1%)
```

### Step 4: Interpretation
Despite the test being 99% accurate, the PPV is only 1% because the disease is very rare (1% prevalence). This means that if the test shows positive, there's only a 1% chance of actually having the disease.

## Why This Happens
- **Disease Rarity**: When the disease is rare, most positive tests are false positives.
- **Test Accuracy**: A test that's 99% accurate (both sensitivity and specificity) still has a high false positive rate when the disease is rare.
- **Mass Testing**: For very rare diseases, mass testing can lead to many false positives, making the test results statistically misleading.

## Example
Imagine a population of 100,000 people:
- 1,000 have the disease (1%)
- 99,000 don't have the disease
- 990 test positive (1% of 1,000)
- 990 test positive (1% of 99,000)
- Total positive tests = 1,980
- True positives = 990
- PPV = 990 / 1,980 = 0.01 (1%)

## Why This Matters
- **False Positives**: For rare diseases, even a highly accurate test can produce many false positives.
- **Statistical Significance**: The disease prevalence has a huge impact on the interpretation of test results.
- **Mass Testing**: For very rare diseases, mass testing can lead to many false positives, making the test results statistically misleading.

## Key Takeaway
When the disease is rare, a positive test result does not mean you have the disease. The probability of having the disease given a positive test is very low (1% in this case). This is why it's important to consider disease prevalence when interpreting test results.
