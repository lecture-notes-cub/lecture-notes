# Gamma-Poisson Bayesian Update

## Key Definitions
- **Poisson Distribution**: $P(X = x | \lambda) = \frac{e^{-\lambda} \lambda^x}{x!}$ [03:12:43–03:13:28]
- **Gamma Distribution**: $\text{Gamma}(\alpha, \beta) = \frac{\beta^\alpha}{\Gamma(\alpha)} \lambda^{\alpha-1} e^{-\beta \lambda}$ [03:17:17–03:17:47]

## Derivation Steps

1. **Joint Probability**: The joint distribution of $X_1, ..., X_n$ and $\lambda$ is:
   $$P(X_1, ..., X_n | \lambda) \cdot P(\lambda) = \left(\prod_{i=1}^n \frac{e^{-\lambda} \lambda^{X_i}}{X_i!}\right) \cdot \frac{\beta^\alpha}{\Gamma(\alpha)} \lambda^{\alpha-1} e^{-\beta \lambda}$$ [03:12:08–03:12:43]

2. **Posterior Distribution**: After simplifying, the posterior distribution of $\lambda$ given $X_1, ..., X_n$ is proportional to:
   $$\lambda^{\alpha + \sum X_i - 1} e^{-(\beta + n)\lambda}$$ [03:17:47–03:18:19]

3. **Final Result**: This is the kernel of a **Gamma distribution** with parameters:
   - $\alpha^* = \alpha + \sum_{i=1}^n X_i$ (updated shape)
   - $\beta^* = \beta + n$ (updated rate) [03:18:19–03:18:48]

## Why This Works
- The posterior is a Gamma distribution because the product of a Poisson likelihood and a Gamma prior results in a conjugate prior.
- The derivation avoids full integration by recognizing the form of the posterior and using the fact that the integral of the posterior kernel over $\lambda$ is 1 (normalization constant).

## Example
- If we have $n=2$ Poisson observations with $X_1=3, X_2=5$ and a prior $\text{Gamma}(\alpha=2, \beta=1)$, the posterior is $\text{Gamma}(\alpha^*=2+8=10, \beta^*=1+2=3)$.

> **Note**: The speaker mentions that the derivation uses the 'Bau's method' (likely a typo for 'Bayes' method) to avoid full computation by focusing on the form of the posterior distribution.
