# Asymptotic Properties of Maximum-Likelihood Estimators

## Consistency

- **Definition**: As $n \to \infty$, the MLE $\hat{\theta}_{\text{ml}}$ converges in probability to the true parameter $\theta$.
  - **Timestamp**: [00:24:25–00:25:07]

- **Reasoning**: The expectation of $\hat{\theta}_{\text{ml}}$ is asymptotically unbiased (i.e., $E[\hat{\theta}_{\text{ml}}] \to \theta$ as $n \to \infty$), and the variance of $\hat{\theta}_{\text{ml}}$ approaches zero.
  - **Timestamp**: [00:25:07–00:25:41]

- **Example**: With larger $n$, the MLE oscillates less around the true value (reducing bias).

## Asymptotic Normality

- **Definition**: For large $n$, $\sqrt{n}(\hat{\theta}_{\text{ml}} - \theta) \xrightarrow{d} N(0, I^{-1}(\theta))$, where $I(\theta)$ is the Fisher information matrix.
  - **Timestamp**: [00:25:41–00:26:16]

- **Implication**: The variance of $\hat{\theta}_{\text{ml}}$ is $\frac{1}{n}I^{-1}(\theta)$, which decreases as $n$ increases.
  - **Timestamp**: [00:25:41–00:26:16]

## Efficiency

- **Definition**: The MLE achieves the Cramér-Rao lower bound for variance among all unbiased estimators.
  - **Timestamp**: [00:26:23–00:26:58]

- **Reasoning**: The Cramér-Rao bound states that $\text{Var}(\hat{\theta}) \geq \frac{1}{nI(\theta)}$. The MLE attains this bound, making it the most efficient unbiased estimator.
  - **Timestamp**: [00:26:23–00:26:58]

- **Example**: The MLE has the smallest possible variance among unbiased estimators (comparable to Heisenberg's uncertainty principle in quantum mechanics).
  - **Timestamp**: [00:26:58–00:27:28]
