# Product Rule and Chain Rule for Joint Distributions

## Definition
The product rule (also called the chain rule) states that for any joint distribution of random variables, the joint probability can be decomposed into a product of conditional probabilities. Specifically, for two variables:

$$P(X, Y) = P(Y | X)P(X) = P(X | Y)P(Y)$$

For $n$ variables, this generalizes to:

$$P(X_1, X_2, ..., X_n) = \prod_{i=1}^{n} P(X_i | X_1, ..., X_{i-1})$$

> **Source**: [00:06:07–00:06:41] (initial definition), [00:06:41–00:07:23] (generalization)

## Alternative Variable Orderings
The product rule allows for different orderings of variables in the decomposition. For example, starting from $X_1$:

$$P(X_1, X_2, ..., X_n) = P(X_1) \cdot P(X_2 | X_1) \cdot P(X_3 | X_2, X_1) \cdot ... \cdot P(X_n | X_{n-1}, ..., X_1)$$

Starting from $X_n$:

$$P(X_1, X_2, ..., X_n) = P(X_n) \cdot P(X_{n-1} | X_n) \cdot P(X_{n-2} | X_{n-1}, X_n) \cdot ... \cdot P(X_1 | X_2, ..., X_n)$$

> **Source**: [00:07:23–00:07:56] (forward and reverse orderings)

## Key Insight
The product rule provides a way to reduce the dimensionality of joint distributions by expressing them in terms of conditional distributions. This is particularly useful in Bayesian networks and probabilistic inference where we want to compute marginal probabilities.

> **Source**: [00:07:56–00:08:35] (discussion of 'product row' and applications)

## Example
Consider three variables $X$, $Y$, $Z$:

- **Forward order**: $P(X) \cdot P(Y | X) \cdot P(Z | X, Y)$
- **Reverse order**: $P(Z) \cdot P(Y | Z) \cdot P(X | Y, Z)$

This flexibility in ordering is crucial for applications where we have different information available at different stages.

> **Source**: [00:07:23–00:07:56] (example of decompositions)

> **Note**: The lecture mentions this as the 'product row' (likely a typo for 'product rule') and emphasizes that there are many decompositions depending on the order of variables.
