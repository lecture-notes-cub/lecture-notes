# Conditional Probability and Independence

## Definition of Conditional Probability

The conditional distribution of $X$ given $Y$ is defined as:

$$P(X | Y) = \frac{P(X, Y)}{P(Y)}$$

This is derived from the joint distribution $P(X, Y)$ divided by the marginal distribution $P(Y)$.

## Intuition Behind Conditional Probability

The conditional distribution shows how the distribution of $X$ changes when we observe $Y$.

## Independence and Conditional Probability

If $X$ and $Y$ are independent, then:

$$P(X, Y) = P(X) \cdot P(Y)$$

Substituting into the conditional probability formula gives:

$$P(X | Y) = \frac{P(X) \cdot P(Y)}{P(Y)} = P(X)$$

This means that observing $Y$ provides no information about $X$ when they are independent.

## Key Insight

When $X$ and $Y$ are dependent, observing $Y$ changes the distribution of $X$.
