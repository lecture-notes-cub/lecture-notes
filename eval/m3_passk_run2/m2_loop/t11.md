# Frequentist vs Bayesian Interpretation of Randomness

## Key Definitions

- **Frequentist View** (classical view):
  - Random variable: A process whose outcome is *unpredictable by definition*
  - This represents *objective uncertainty* (the randomness is inherent in the process)
  - Timestamp: [00:34:56–00:35:40]

- **Bayesian View**:
  - Random variable: The result of a *completely deterministic process* whose outcome is unknown due to *subjective ignorance*
  - This represents *subjective uncertainty* (the randomness is due to lack of information)
  - Timestamp: [00:35:40–00:36:16]

## Example: Tosca Coin

- **Frequentist perspective**:
  - The coin toss outcome is random by definition
  - The outcome is unpredictable (as per classical view)
  - Timestamp: [00:36:16–00:36:52]

- **Bayesian perspective**:
  - The coin toss outcome is *predictable in principle* (if we knew initial conditions)
  - The randomness is due to *lack of information* about the physical process
  - Timestamp: [00:36:53–00:37:23]

## Example: Eros Eins Train Arrival

- **Frequentist perspective**:
  - Arrival time is a random variable with a distribution that can be estimated through statistics
  - The randomness is inherent in the process (e.g., traffic conditions)
  - Timestamp: [00:37:23–00:37:53]

- **Bayesian perspective**:
  - The arrival time is *predictable* if we have complete information (e.g., traffic conditions)
  - The randomness is due to *subjective ignorance* (lack of information about current conditions)
  - Timestamp: [00:37:53–00:38:26]

- **Updating with new information**:
  - When new information is observed (e.g., train delay on screen), the distribution of the random variable changes
  - This reflects *subjective ignorance* being updated
  - Timestamp: [00:38:26–00:38:54]

- **Conclusion**:
  - The randomness is *not objective* but *subjective* (depends on the observer's information)
  - Timestamp: [00:38:54–00:39:23]
