# Sample Size vs. Parameter Count and Bayesian Updating

## Key Concepts

### Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) and Sample Size

- **Condition for MLE Validity**: MLE is justified when the sample size $n$ is not much larger than the parameter dimension $d$ (i.e., $n \gg d$). This is the condition where MLE provides a reasonable estimate without strong theoretical guarantees.
  - [00:40:46–00:41:49]

- **Overfitting in Machine Learning**:
  - When $n$ is comparable to $d$ (i.e., $n \approx d$), models exhibit overfitting (gap between training and validation error).
  - When $n \gg d$, models generalize well (no gap between training and validation error).
  - [00:43:19–00:43:49]

### Bayesian Updating with No Observations

- **Posterior Distribution at $n=0$**:
  - When there are no observations ($n=0$), the posterior distribution equals the prior distribution.
  - This is because Bayesian updating requires observations to change the prior.
  - [00:45:58–00:46:28]

- **Bayesian Framework Flexibility**:
  - Bayesian methods work for any number of observations, including $n=0$.
  - With one observation, the posterior distribution updates the prior.
  - [00:45:58–00:46:28]
