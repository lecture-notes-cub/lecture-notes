## Sequential Bayesian Combination in Elephant Example

### [00:47:09–00:47:40] Problem Setup
- **Blind wisdomers**: Group of individuals with different sensory capabilities (e.g., touch, sound, smell) tasked with estimating an unknown animal's mass.
- **Prior distribution**: Initial belief about animal mass with restricted support: $ \theta \in (0, 30 \text{ tons}] $ (since blue whale is the heaviest at ~30 tons).

### [00:47:40–00:48:13] First Wisdom (Tactile Observation)
- **Probabilistic model**: $ P(x | \theta) $, where $ x $ = tactile observation (e.g., feeling elephant's nose).
- **Bayesian update**: Computes posterior $ P(\theta | x) $ using:
  $$
  P(\theta | x) \propto P(x | \theta) \cdot P(\theta)
  $$
- **Example**: After touching elephant's nose, posterior suggests either a very large animal or a snake-like animal.

### [00:48:44–00:49:16] Second Wisdom (Acoustic Observation)
- **New probabilistic model**: $ P(y | \theta) $, where $ y $ = acoustic observation (e.g., sounds).
- **Key distinction**: $ y $ lives in a different space than $ x $ (tactile vs. acoustic).

### [00:49:51–00:50:27] Combining Posterior from First Wisdom
- **Posterior as new prior**: Uses $ P(\theta | x) $ from first wisdom as the new prior for second wisdom.
- **Joint posterior**: $ P(\theta | x, y) \propto P(y | \theta) \cdot P(\theta | x) $.
- **Normalization**: Result normalized to form a valid posterior distribution.

### [00:50:56–00:51:26] Iterative Combination
- **Process**: Repeatedly combine new observations with existing posterior (as new prior).
- **Result**: After multiple iterations, posterior becomes sharply concentrated around a specific mass.

### [00:52:06–00:52:43] Advantages of Bayesian Framework
- **Combining models**: Integrates multiple probabilistic models (e.g., tactile, acoustic) into a unified posterior.
- **Modularity**: Builds complex models from simpler components ("building blocks").
