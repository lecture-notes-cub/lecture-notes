## Frequentist vs Bayesian Interpretation of Randomness (00:34:56–00:39:24)

### Key Definitions

- **Frequentist View** (classical): A random variable is a process whose outcome is *unpredictable by definition*. This represents objective uncertainty.

- **Bayesian View**: A random variable is the result of a *completely deterministic process* whose outcome is unknown due to *subjective ignorance* (lack of information). This is not objective uncertainty.

### Example: Tosca Coin

- **Frequentist**: The coin toss outcome is random (unpredictable).
- **Bayesian**: The outcome is *deterministic* but *unpredictable due to lack of information* (e.g., initial conditions, mechanics).

### Example: Eros Eins Train Arrival

- **Observation**: Train arrives at 4 minutes past 10, but actual arrival time varies (e.g., 5–10–30 minutes late).
- **Frequentist**: Arrival time is a random variable with a predictable distribution (e.g., empirical density function).
- **Bayesian**: Arrival time is *deterministic* but *subject to ignorance* (e.g., traffic conditions). 
  - *Ignorance changes with new information*: When the screen shows a 5-minute delay, the distribution updates (e.g., now likely 9–10 minutes past 10).

### Critical Distinction

- **Frequentist**: Randomness = *objective unpredictability* (inherent in the process).
- **Bayesian**: Randomness = *subjective ignorance* (lack of information about a deterministic process).
