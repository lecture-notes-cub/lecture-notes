## Product Rule and Chain Rule (00:06:07–00:08:35)

### Key Definitions

- **Joint Distribution Decomposition**: For two random variables $X$ and $Y$, the joint distribution $P(X, Y)$ can be expressed as:
  $$
P(X, Y) = P(X | Y)P(Y) = P(Y | X)P(X)$$
  This is a *decomposition of joint distribution* into conditional and marginal distributions.

- **Product Rule (Chain Rule)**: For $n$ random variables $X_1, X_2, ..., X_n$, the joint distribution can be decomposed as:
  $$
P(X_1, X_2, ..., X_n) = \prod_{i=1}^{n} P(X_i | X_{i+1}, ..., X_n)$$
  where $P(X_n | \emptyset) = P(X_n)$ (marginal distribution).

### Alternative Variable Orderings

The product rule allows for *arbitrary ordering* of variables in the decomposition. For example:

- **Forward Order** (standard): $P(X_1 | X_2, ..., X_n) \cdot P(X_2 | X_3, ..., X_n) \cdot ... \cdot P(X_{n-1} | X_n) \cdot P(X_n)$
- **Reverse Order** (alternative): $P(X_n | X_1, ..., X_{n-1}) \cdot P(X_{n-1} | X_1, ..., X_{n-2}) \cdot ... \cdot P(X_2 | X_1) \cdot P(X_1)$
