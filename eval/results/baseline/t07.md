## Maximum-Likelihood Objective and Log Likelihood (00:19:48–00:22:03)

### Key Definitions
- **Likelihood Function**: Product of probability density functions (PDFs) evaluated at observed data points $x_1, \dots, x_n$, where the data is assumed to be i.i.d. from a distribution parameterized by $\theta$.
  - $\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \prod_{i=1}^n f(x_i | \theta)$
- **Log Likelihood**: Logarithm of the likelihood function, $\ell(\theta) = \ln \mathcal{L}(\theta)$

### Why Log Likelihood is Used
1. **Computational Convenience**:
   - The likelihood function is a product of $n$ terms, which is difficult to maximize directly due to numerical instability and complexity.
   - Taking the logarithm converts the product into a sum: $\ell(\theta) = \sum_{i=1}^n \ln f(x_i | \theta)$
   - Sums are easier to maximize than products (e.g., via differentiation, gradient-based optimization).

2. **Mathematical Properties**:
   - The logarithm is a monotonically increasing function, so maximizing $\ell(\theta)$ is equivalent to maximizing $\mathcal{L}(\theta)$.
   - This avoids numerical underflow issues when dealing with very small probabilities.

### Example
- For i.i.d. data $x_1, \dots, x_n$ from a distribution with PDF $f(x | \theta)$, the likelihood function is $\prod_{i=1}^n f(x_i | \theta)$, and the log likelihood is $\sum_{i=1}^n \ln f(x_i | \theta)$.
