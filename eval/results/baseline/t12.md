## Sample Size vs. Parameter Count and Bayesian Updating

### Key Concepts

- **Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE)**: In classical statistics, MLE provides a point estimate. It is justified when $n$ (number of observations) is not much larger than $d$ (dimension of parameters), but $n$ is much larger than $d$.

- **Overfitting in Machine Learning**: When $n \leq d$ (number of observations ≤ number of parameters), models overfit (train error ≠ validation error). When $n \gg d$, models generalize well (train error ≈ validation error).

- **Historical Context**: Early deep neural networks (small models + large datasets) avoided overfitting because MLE was optimal. As models grew larger ($n \not\gg d$), overfitting became prevalent.

### Bayesian Framework

- **Bayesian Updating with $n = 0$**: When no observations are available ($n = 0$), the posterior distribution equals the prior distribution (e.g., uniform distribution over the parameter space).

- **Posterior Update**: With $n = 1$, one observation updates the prior to form a posterior distribution.
