## Bayes' Theorem: Key Concepts

### Prior
[00:15:28–00:16:01] The prior distribution reflects our initial knowledge about the variable of interest (Y) before observing any data.

### Likelihood
[00:16:45–00:17:14] The likelihood function expresses the probability of observing the data given the parameter values.

### Posterior
[00:16:01–00:16:45] The posterior distribution is the updated probability distribution of the variable of interest after incorporating new evidence.

### Evidence
[00:17:14–00:17:43] The evidence is the normalization constant that ensures the posterior distribution integrates to 1. It represents the total probability of the observed data.

### Bayes' Theorem Formula
[00:16:01–00:16:45] $ p(Y|X) = \frac{p(X|Y) \cdot p(Y)}{p(X)} $, where:
- $ p(Y|X) $: Posterior probability
- $ p(X|Y) $: Likelihood
- $ p(Y) $: Prior probability
- $ p(X) $: Evidence (normalization constant)

### Conceptual Explanation
[00:16:45–00:17:43] Bayes' theorem updates prior knowledge with new observations to form posterior knowledge. It solves inverse probabilistic problems by transforming prior beliefs into posterior beliefs based on observed data.
