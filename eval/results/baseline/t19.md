#### Final Explanation (02:00:38–02:02:52)
- **Correct formula for positive predictive value (PPV)**:
  
  $$
  \text{PPV} = \frac{\text{True positives}}{\text{True positives} + \text{False positives}}
  $$

  - **Given**:
    - Disease prevalence = 1% (implied by `10⁻²`)
    - Test sensitivity = 99% (true positive rate)
    - Test specificity = 99% (true negative rate)

- **Calculation**:
  - False positive rate = 1 - specificity = 0.01
  - True positives = 0.99 × 0.01 = 0.0099
  - False positives = 0.01 × 0.99 = 0.0099
  - PPV = 0.0099 / (0.0099 + 0.0099) = 0.5

- **Key Insight**:
  - Even with a 99% accurate test, PPV is only 50% for a rare disease (1% prevalence)
  - *Source*: [02:01:17–02:02:52]

### Critical Clarifications
- **Why PPV is low**:
  - Low disease prevalence means most positive tests are false positives
  - *Source*: [02:01:50–02:02:22]

- **Real-world implication**:
  - Mass testing for rare diseases can produce misleading results
