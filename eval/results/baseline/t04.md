## Marginalization and Expectation Interpretation

### Key Definitions

- **Marginalization**: The process of integrating out (or summing over) a variable from a joint distribution to obtain the distribution of the remaining variables.
- **Expectation Interpretation**: The marginal distribution of a variable can be viewed as the expectation of the conditional distribution over the other variables.

### Core Reasoning

The lecture explains marginalization through the context of conditional probability distributions. Specifically, it states:

> "The integral of conditional density, $P(y \mid x)$ with respect to $y$ results in one, which means we obtain $1 = P(x \mid y) \cdot P(y)$" [00:09:08–00:10:00]

This leads to the marginal distribution formula:

$$P(x) = \int P(x \mid y) P(y) \, dy$$

The lecture emphasizes that this integral represents an expectation over $y$:

> "This is the same as to say that this is expectation with respect to $y$ over all conditional distributions. We take all different conditional distributions on $x$ given different $y$'s, we average them out with respect to $y$ and we obtain unconditional distribution on $x$" [00:11:17–00:11:49]

### Physical Interpretation

The marginal distribution $P(x)$ can be interpreted as:

- A weighted average of conditional distributions $P(x \mid y)$, where the weights are the marginal probabilities $P(y)$.
- This is equivalent to integrating the joint distribution $P(x, y)$ over $y$.

### Generalization

The lecture notes that this concept extends to multiple variables:

> "By induction it's straightforward to prove that this goes through for arbitrary number of random variables so you have say distribution over $x$ and $x_1$ to $x_n$ and we would like to get distribution over $x_1$ to $x_k$ we can easily do it just by taking joint distribution and integrating out remaining variables" [00:11:49–00:12:44]
