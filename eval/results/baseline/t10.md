## Bayesian Parameter Estimation vs. Point Estimates

### [00:32:13–00:32:53] Key Definitions
- **Bayesian inference** starts from a known probability distribution of observations $X$ parameterized by a parameter $\theta$ (written as $P(X | \theta)$).
- **Prior distribution**: Reflects initial knowledge/preference about possible values of $\theta$.
- **Posterior distribution**: Result of applying Bayes' theorem to update prior knowledge with observed data $X_1, ..., X_n$.

### [00:32:53–00:33:37] Estimation Process
- **Frequentist approach**: Uses maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) to find a point estimate of $\theta$.
- **Bayesian approach**: Derives a posterior distribution $P(\theta | X_1, ..., X_n)$ via Bayes' theorem:
  $$
  P(\theta | X) = \frac{P(X | \theta) \cdot P(\theta)}{P(X)}
  $$
  where $P(X)$ is the marginal likelihood (normalization constant).

### [00:33:37–00:34:13] Posterior Distribution
- **Posterior derivation**: Product of likelihood $P(X_i | \theta)$ (for independent observations) and prior $P(\theta)$, renormalized by $P(X)$.
- **Key difference**: Bayesian method yields a **distribution** (uncertainty about $\theta$) vs. frequentist point estimate.

### [00:34:14–00:34:56] Critical Distinction
- **Frequentist**: Point estimate (e.g., MLE).
- **Bayesian**: Full posterior distribution (captures uncertainty).
