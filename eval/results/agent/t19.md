# Rare Disease Test Calculation Correction

## Key Definitions
- **True Positive Rate (Sensitivity)**: Probability of testing positive given disease presence (P(positive | disease))
- **False Positive Rate**: Probability of testing positive given no disease (P(positive | no disease))
- **Disease Prevalence**: Proportion of population with disease (here: 0.0001)

## Student Error vs. Correct Calculation

### Student's Initial Error
- **Incorrect Formula**: `99 × 10⁻⁴ / (0.99 × 10⁻⁴ + 0.99 × (1 - 10⁻⁴))`
- **Why it's wrong**: Student used P(positive | disease) = 0.99 for the negative disease case (P(positive | no disease) = 0.99), but the instructor clarifies this is a misunderstanding of conditional probability.
- **Timestamp**: [01:58:08–01:58:38]

### Corrected Calculation
- **True Positive Rate**: 0.99 (P(positive | disease))
- **True Negative Rate**: 0.99 (P(negative | no disease))
- **Disease Prevalence**: 0.0001 (1 in 10,000)
- **Correct Formula**: `P(disease | positive) = [P(positive | disease) × P(disease)] / [P(positive | disease) × P(disease) + P(positive | no disease) × P(no disease)]`
- **Calculation**: `(0.99 × 0.0001) / (0.99 × 0.0001 + 0.01 × 0.9999) = 0.0099 / 0.019899 ≈ 0.00498` → **~0.5%**
- **Timestamp**: [02:01:17–02:01:50]

## Why the Result is Counterintuitive
- **Disease Rarity**: With disease prevalence at 0.0001, even a 99% accurate test yields only ~0.5% true positive rate when test is positive.
- **Key Insight**: The low prevalence means false positives dominate the positive results.
- **Timestamp**: [02:01:50–02:02:22]

## Critical Clarification
- **P(positive | no disease)**: Student incorrectly assumed this was 0.99, but it's actually the false positive rate (0.01 for this example).
- **Timestamp**: [01:58:38–02:00:08]
