# Marginalization and Expectation Interpretation

## Key Concepts

- **Marginalization**: The process of integrating out (summing over) a variable to obtain the distribution of the remaining variables. In this context, it refers to computing the marginal distribution of $x$ from the joint distribution $P(x, y)$.
  [00:09:08–00:10:00]

- **Expectation Interpretation**: The marginal distribution $P(x)$ can be interpreted as the expectation of the conditional distribution $P(x|y)$ over all possible values of $y$. This is expressed as:
  $$
  P(x) = \int P(x|y) P(y) \, dy
  $$
  [00:10:02–00:10:46]

## Derivation and Physical Meaning

The lecture explains that integrating the conditional distribution $P(x|y)$ with respect to $y$ gives the marginal distribution $P(x)$. This is equivalent to taking an expectation over $y$:

- **Mathematical Expression**:
  $$
  P(x) = \int P(x|y) P(y) \, dy
  $$
  [00:10:46–00:11:17]

- **Physical Meaning**:
  - Imagine knowing all conditional distributions $P(x|y)$ for different $y$ values.
  - The marginal distribution $P(x)$ is obtained by averaging these conditional distributions over all $y$.
  - This is also equivalent to integrating the joint distribution $P(x, y)$ over $y$ (i.e., 'integrating out' $y$).
  [00:11:17–00:11:49]

## Example

The lecture mentions that this concept extends to multiple variables. For example, if we have a joint distribution over $x_1, x_2, ..., x_n$, we can obtain the marginal distribution over $x_1, ..., x_k$ by integrating out the remaining variables.
