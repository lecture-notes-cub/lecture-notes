# Product Rule and Chain Rule

## Product Rule (Product Row)

The product rule states that for any joint distribution $P(X_1, X_2, ..., X_n)$, it can be decomposed into a product of one-dimensional conditional distributions:

$$P(X_1, X_2, ..., X_n) = P(X_1 | X_2, ..., X_n) \cdot P(X_2 | X_3, ..., X_n) \cdot ... \cdot P(X_{n-1} | X_n) \cdot P(X_n)$$

This is a **chain rule** decomposition where the variables are ordered from $X_1$ to $X_n$.

## Alternative Variable Orderings

The product rule can be applied with different variable orderings. For example, if we reverse the order:

$$P(X_1, X_2, ..., X_n) = P(X_n | X_1, X_2, ..., X_{n-1}) \cdot P(X_{n-1} | X_1, ..., X_{n-2}) \cdot ... \cdot P(X_2 | X_1) \cdot P(X_1)$$

This shows that the chain rule is not unique to a specific ordering of variables.

## Key Points

- The product rule is a fundamental identity for decomposing joint distributions into conditional probabilities.
- The chain rule can be written in multiple ways depending on the order of variables.
- This decomposition is useful for reducing variance and understanding dependencies between variables.
