# Sequential Bayesian Observation Combination in Elephant Example

## Key Setup
- **Prior Distribution**: Initial belief about animal mass (positive semi-axis, capped at 30 tons for blue whale)
- **Sensors**: Blind wisdomers use different senses (touch, sound, smell) to observe the animal
- **Objective**: Estimate the animal's mass using sequential Bayesian updates

## Sequential Bayesian Update Process

### Step 1: First Observation (Touch)
- **Observation**: Touching the elephant's nose
- **Model**: Probabilistic model for tactile observations: $P(x | \theta)$
- **Update**: Bayesian inference produces posterior distribution $P(\theta | x)$
  - *Example*: After touching the nose, the posterior suggests either a very large animal (elephant) or a snake-like animal
  - **Timestamp**: [00:49:16–00:49:51]

### Step 2: Second Observation (Sound)
- **Observation**: Listening to the animal's sounds
- **Model**: Probabilistic model for acoustic observations: $P(y | \theta)$
- **Update**: Bayesian inference combines prior (posterior from Step 1) with new likelihood
  - *Example*: Uses posterior from Step 1 as new prior
  - **Timestamp**: [00:50:56–00:51:26]

### Sequential Combination
- **Process**: Repeatedly apply Bayesian update using posterior from previous step as new prior
- **Result**: Converges to a sharp posterior distribution around a specific mass
- **Timestamp**: [00:52:06–00:52:43]

## Why This Works
- **Advantage**: Combines different probabilistic models (sensors) into a single coherent estimate
- **Key Insight**: Bayesian framework allows building complex models from simple components (like 'building blocks')
- **Timestamp**: [00:52:43–00:53:21]

> **Note**: The elephant example illustrates sequential Bayesian inference where each observation updates the posterior distribution. The final estimate becomes more precise as more observations are incorporated.
