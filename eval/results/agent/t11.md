# Frequentist vs Bayesian Interpretation of Randomness

## Key Definitions

- **Frequentist View** (Classical):
  - A random variable is a process whose outcome is *unpredictable by definition*.
  - [00:34:56–00:35:40]

- **Bayesian View**:
  - A random variable is the *result of a completely deterministic process* whose outcome is unknown to us only because we left some information which determines the outcome of this process.
  - [00:35:40–00:36:16]

## Core Reasoning

- **Frequentist**: Randomness is *inherent* in the process (e.g., coin toss). Even with full knowledge of initial conditions, outcomes are unpredictable.
  - [00:36:16–00:36:52]

- **Bayesian**: Randomness is *epistemic* (due to missing information). With more data, uncertainty decreases (e.g., train arrival time updates when screen shows delay).
  - [00:38:54–00:39:23]

## Examples

1. **Coin Toss**:
   - Frequentist: Coin toss is inherently random (unpredictable by definition).
     - [00:34:56–00:35:40]
   - Bayesian: Coin toss is deterministic (physics determines outcome), but we lack initial conditions (how it's tossed).
     - [00:36:16–00:36:52]

2. **Train Arrival**:
   - Frequentist: Arrival time is random (unpredictable without full knowledge).
     - [00:37:23–00:37:53]
   - Bayesian: Arrival time is predictable with full information (e.g., traffic conditions), but current uncertainty is *subjective* (e.g., screen shows 5-min delay).
     - [00:38:54–00:39:23]

## Critical Distinction

- **Objective vs. Subjective Uncertainty**:
  - Frequentist: Uncertainty is *objective* (the process is truly random).
  - Bayesian: Uncertainty is *subjective* (due to incomplete information).
