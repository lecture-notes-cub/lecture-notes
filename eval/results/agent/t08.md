## Asymptotic Properties of Maximum-Likelihood Estimators

### 1. Asymptotic Unbiasedness

- **Definition**: As $n \to \infty$, the expectation of the MLE $\hat{\theta}_{\text{ml}}$ converges to the true parameter $\theta$.
  - **Timestamp**: [00:22:53–00:23:38]

- **Key Reasoning**: The MLE is a random variable (since it's a function of random observations $X_1, \dots, X_n$). As sample size increases, the bias diminishes, making the estimator asymptotically unbiased.

### 2. Consistency

- **Definition**: $\lim_{n \to \infty} \hat{\theta}_{\text{ml}} = \theta$ almost surely.
  - **Timestamp**: [00:24:25–00:25:07]

- **Key Reasoning**: The MLE converges to the true parameter value as the number of observations $n$ increases. This follows from asymptotic unbiasedness and the decreasing variance with $n$.

### 3. Asymptotic Normality

- **Definition**: For large $n$, $\sqrt{n}(\hat{\theta}_{\text{ml}} - \theta) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0, \mathcal{I}(\theta)^{-1})$.
  - **Timestamp**: [00:25:07–00:25:41]

- **Key Reasoning**: The MLE has an asymptotic normal distribution with mean $\theta$ and variance $\mathcal{I}(\theta)^{-1}/n$, where $\mathcal{I}(\theta)$ is the Fisher information matrix.

### 4. Efficiency

- **Definition**: The MLE achieves the Cramér-Rao lower bound for variance among all unbiased estimators.
  - **Timestamp**: [00:26:23–00:27:28]

- **Key Reasoning**: The Cramér-Rao bound states that $\text{Var}(\hat{\theta}) \geq \frac{1}{n \mathcal{I}(\theta)}$. The MLE attains this bound, making it the most efficient unbiased estimator.

### Summary of Key Properties

| Property | Condition | Implication |
|----------|------------|--------------|
| Asymptotic Unbiasedness | $n \to \infty$ | $\mathbb{E}[\hat{\theta}_{\text{ml}}] \to \theta$ |
| Consistency | $n \to \infty$ | $\hat{\theta}_{\text{ml}} \to \theta$ a.s. |
| Asymptotic Normality | Large $n$ | $\sqrt{n}(\hat{\theta}_{\text{ml}} - \theta) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0, \mathcal{I}(\theta)^{-1})$ |
| Efficiency | Unbiased estimators | $\text{Var}(\hat{\theta}_{\text{ml}}) = \frac{1}{n \mathcal{I}(\theta)}$ |

**Note**: The lecture emphasizes that the MLE is optimal under these asymptotic conditions. The Cramér-Rao bound (mentioned as 'Raul Karamer bound') is a key theoretical foundation for efficiency.
