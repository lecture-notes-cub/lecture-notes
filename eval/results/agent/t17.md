# Bayesian Alarm Example: Posterior Calculations and Prior Effects

## Key Definitions
- **Posterior Probability**: Probability of a burglary given evidence (alarm signal)
- **Prior Probability**: Initial belief about burglary likelihood (before evidence)
- **Burglary Prior (P(t=1))**: Probability of burglary before evidence (e.g., 0.02)
- **Alarm Probability (P(E|t=1))**: Probability of alarm given burglary (e.g., 0.9)
- **Alarm Probability (P(E|t=0))**: Probability of alarm given no burglary (e.g., 0.01)

## Initial Posterior Calculation (Prior = 0.02)
- **Prior**: P(t=1) = 0.02
- **Evidence**: Alarm triggered (E)
- **Posterior**: P(t=1|E) = 70% (0.7)
  - *Calculation*: 1 × (0.02 × 10⁻⁴) / [2 × 10⁻⁴ + 10⁻³] ≈ 0.7
  - *Timestamp*: [01:19:19–01:19:53]

## Effect of Increasing Burglary Prior (10×)
- **New Prior**: P(t=1) = 0.2 (10× original)
- **New Posterior**: P(t=1|E) = 67% (0.67)
  - *Calculation*: 1 × (0.2 × 10⁻³) / [2 × 10⁻³ + 10⁻³] = 2/3 ≈ 0.67
  - *Timestamp*: [01:22:42–01:23:13]
- **Key Insight**: Posterior increases **4×** (from 70% → 67%) when prior increases **10×**
  - *Why?* Because posterior is bounded by 100% (can't exceed 1)
  - *Timestamp*: [01:23:13–01:23:46]

## Why Posterior Doesn't Scale Linearly with Prior
- **Critical Limitation**: Posterior probability cannot exceed 100%
- **Example**: If prior increases by 10× (from 2% → 20%), posterior increases by only 4× (70% → 67%)
- *Why?* The denominator (total probability) adjusts to keep posterior ≤ 1
- *Timestamp*: [01:21:37–01:22:06]
