# Asymptotic Properties of Maximum-Likelihood Estimators

## Consistency

- **Definition**: As $n \to \infty$, the MLE $\hat{\theta}_{\text{ml}}$ converges in probability to the true parameter $\theta$.
  - [00:24:25–00:25:07]

- **Clarification**: The lecturer emphasizes that the MLE is *asymptotically unbiased* (not consistent) in the early part of the lecture.
  - [00:22:53–00:23:38]

- **Reasoning**: The MLE is a random variable (since it's a function of random observations $X_1, \dots, X_n$). As $n$ increases, the MLE oscillates around the true value with decreasing amplitude.
  - [00:23:39–00:24:25]

## Asymptotic Normality

- **Definition**: For large $n$, $\sqrt{n}(\hat{\theta}_{\text{ml}} - \theta) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0, I^{-1}(\theta))$, where $I(\theta)$ is the Fisher information matrix.
  - [00:25:41–00:26:16]

- **Variance**: $\text{Var}(\hat{\theta}_{\text{}ml}) \approx \frac{1}{n} I^{-1}(\theta)$
  - [00:25:41–00:26:16]

- **Implication**: Doubling $n$ reduces variance by a factor of 2.
  - [00:26:23–00:26:58]

## Efficiency

- **Definition**: The MLE achieves the Cramér-Rao lower bound for variance among all unbiased estimators.
  - [00:26:58–00:27:28]

- **Cramér-Rao Bound**: $\text{Var}(\hat{\theta}) \geq \frac{1}{n I(\theta)}$ for unbiased $\hat{\theta}$.
  - [00:26:58–00:27:28]

- **Key Insight**: The MLE has the smallest possible variance among unbiased estimators, making it efficient.
  - [00:27:28–00:27:59]

  - [00:25:41–00:26:16]
