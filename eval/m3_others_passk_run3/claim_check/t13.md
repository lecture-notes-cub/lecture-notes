- **Prior Distribution**: Restricted support on animal mass (positive semi-axis, max 30 tons for blue whale)

- **Goal**: Estimate animal mass through sequential Bayesian updates

## Sequential Bayesian Updating Process

### First Observation (Touch)
- **Timestamp**: [00:49:16–00:49:51]
- **Action**: One wisdomer touches the elephant's nose
- **Model**: Probabilistic model for tactile observations: $P(x | \theta)$
- **Update**: Bayesian inference produces posterior distribution $P(\theta | x)$
- **Result**: After touching nose, posterior suggests either a very large animal or a snake-like animal

### Second Observation (Sound)
- **Timestamp**: [00:50:27–00:50:56]
- **Action**: Another wisdomer listens to the animal's sounds
- **Model**: Probabilistic model for acoustic observations: $P(y | \theta)$
- **Update**: Uses posterior from first observation as new prior
- **Result**: Combines information from two sources into single posterior

### Sequential Combination
- **Timestamp**: [00:51:26–00:52:06]
- **Process**: For each new observation:
  - Take previous posterior as new prior
  - Multiply by current likelihood
  - Normalize to get new posterior
- **Result**: After multiple observations, posterior becomes sharply concentrated around true mass

## Key Insight
- **Bayesian Advantage**: Can combine different probabilistic models (sensors) into a more complex model
- **Example**: Building a robust mass estimate from multiple sensory inputs
- **Critical Limitation**: Requires access to posterior distributions from previous steps, not raw observations
