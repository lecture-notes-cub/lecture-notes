# Conditional Probability and Independence

## Definition of Conditional Distribution

The conditional distribution of $X$ given $Y$ is defined as:

$$P(X | Y) = \frac{P(X, Y)}{P(Y)}$$

This is derived from the joint distribution $P(X, Y)$ and the marginal distribution $P(Y)$.

## Intuition and Independence Case

When $X$ and $Y$ are independent, the conditional distribution simplifies to the marginal distribution of $X$:

$$P(X | Y) = P(X)$$

This occurs because the joint distribution factors as $P(X) \cdot P(Y)$, so dividing by $P(Y)$ leaves $P(X)$.

## Key Insight

- **Independence**: If $X$ and $Y$ are independent, observing $Y$ provides no information about $X$.
- **Dependence**: If $X$ and $Y$ are dependent, observing $Y$ changes the distribution of $X$.
