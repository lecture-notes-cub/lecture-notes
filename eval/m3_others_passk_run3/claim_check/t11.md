# Frequentist vs Bayesian Interpretation of Randomness

## Key Distinction

- **Frequentist view**: Randomness is *objective* and *unpredictable by definition*. A random variable is a process whose outcome is inherently unpredictable (e.g., coin tosses).
  - *Citation*: [00:34:56–00:35:40]

- **Bayesian view**: Randomness is *subjective ignorance* from a deterministic process. The outcome is predictable in principle but unknown due to missing information (e.g., train arrival time).
  - *Citation*: [00:35:40–00:36:16]

## Examples

### Coin Toss Example
- A coin toss is *random* in frequentist terms because it's inherently unpredictable.
  - *Citation*: [00:36:16–00:36:52]

- In Bayesian terms, the coin toss is *deterministic* (physics governs the outcome) but we lack information to predict it.
  - *Citation*: [00:36:53–00:37:23]

### Train Arrival Example
- Train arrival time is a *random variable* in frequentist terms (statistically variable).
  - *Citation*: [00:37:23–00:37:53]

- In Bayesian terms, the train's arrival time is *predictable in principle* with more information (e.g., traffic conditions).
  - *Citation*: [00:38:26–00:38:54]

## Core Difference

- **Frequentist**: Uncertainty is *objective* (the process is inherently random).
- **Bayesian**: Uncertainty is *subjective* (due to incomplete information about a deterministic process).
  - *Citation*: [00:38:54–00:39:23]

> **Note**: The lecture emphasizes that Bayesian uncertainty is *changeable* with new information (e.g., train delay screen updates the distribution).
