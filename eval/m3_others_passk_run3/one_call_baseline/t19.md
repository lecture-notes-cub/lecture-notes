### Key Definitions
- **Positive test condition on negative disease**: Probability of testing positive when disease is absent (false positive rate)
- **Negative test condition on positive disease**: Probability of testing negative when disease is present (false negative rate)

#### Final Explanation (02:00:38–02:02:52)
- **Correct formula for positive predictive value (PPV)**:
  
  $$
  \text{PPV} = \frac{\text{True positives}}{\text{True positives} + \text{False positives}}
  $$

  With disease prevalence `P(D) = 0.01` and test accuracy:
  - `P(Positive | D) = 0.99` (true positive rate)
  - `P(Negative | ¬D) = 0.99` (true negative rate) → `P(Positive | ¬D) = 0.01` (false positive rate)

- **Calculation**:
  
  $$
  \text{PPV} = \frac{0.99 \times 0.01}{0.99 \times 0.01 + 0.01 \times 0.99} = \frac{0.0099}{0.0198} = 0.05
  $$

  *Note*: The ASR states `0.01` (1%) but the calculation shows `0.05` (5%). This discrepancy is marked as **unclear**.

- **Key Insight**:
  - A test with `99%` accuracy can have a low PPV (e.g., `5%`) for rare diseases due to low prevalence.
  - *Example*: If disease prevalence is `1%`, `1%` of people with positive tests actually have the disease.
