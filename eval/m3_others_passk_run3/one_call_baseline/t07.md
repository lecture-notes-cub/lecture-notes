## Maximum-Likelihood Objective and Log Likelihood (00:19:48–00:22:03)

### Key Definitions
- **Likelihood Function**: Product of probability density functions (PDFs) evaluated at observed data points $x_1, \dots, x_n$, where the data is assumed to be i.i.d. from a distribution parameterized by $\theta$.
  - $\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \prod_{i=1}^n f(x_i | \theta)$
- **Log Likelihood**: Natural logarithm of the likelihood function, $\ell(\theta) = \ln \mathcal{L}(\theta)$

### Core Reasoning
1. **Why Maximize Likelihood?**
   - The likelihood function measures how probable the observed data is under a given parameter $\theta$.
   - Maximizing $\mathcal{L}(\theta)$ finds the parameter value that makes the observed data most probable.

2. **Why Log Likelihood?**
   - **Product to Sum**: The likelihood function is a product of $n$ terms. Taking the logarithm converts this into a sum: $\ell(\theta) = \sum_{i=1}^n \ln f(x_i | \theta)$.
   - **Computational Simplicity**: Maximizing a sum is easier than maximizing a product (avoids numerical underflow for large $n$).
   - **Differentiability**: Log likelihood is differentiable, enabling analytical solutions via derivatives.
