## Bayesian Parameter Estimation vs. Point Estimates

### [00:32:13–00:32:53] Key Definitions
- **Bayesian inference** starts from a prior distribution reflecting knowledge/preference about parameter $\theta$.
- $P(X | \theta)$: Probability density function for observations $X$ given parameter $\theta$ (equivalent to $P(\theta | X)$ in Bayesian context).
- **Observations**: $X_1, X_2, ..., X_n$ (independent and identically distributed).

### [00:32:53–00:33:37] Estimation Process
- **Prior distribution**: Initial belief about $\theta$ (e.g., from prior knowledge).
- **Posterior distribution**: $P(\theta | X_1, ..., X_n)$ derived via Bayes' theorem:
  $$
  P(\theta | X) \propto P(X | \theta) \cdot P(\theta)
  $$
  (where $P(\theta)$ is the prior, and $P(X | \theta)$ is the likelihood).
- **Bayes' theorem application**: Multiply likelihood with prior and renormalize to obtain posterior.

### [00:33:37–00:34:13] Contrast with Point Estimates
- **Frequentist approach**: Uses maximum likelihood estimation (MLE), yielding a single point estimate.
- **Bayesian approach**: Yields a **posterior distribution** (not a point estimate), capturing uncertainty in $\theta$.

### [00:34:14–00:34:56] Key Difference
- **Frequentist**: Point estimate (e.g., MLE).
- **Bayesian**: Full distribution of $\theta$ (posterior).
