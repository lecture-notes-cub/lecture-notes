# Bayesian Alarm Example: Posterior Calculations and Prior Effects

## Key Definitions
- **Prior Probability (P(t=1))**: Initial belief that a burglar is present (e.g., 0.1 before update)
- **Posterior Probability (P(t=1|E))**: Updated belief after observing evidence (e.g., alarm signal)
- **Bayesian Update**: Process of adjusting prior with new evidence to get posterior

- **Posterior**: P(t=1|E) = 70% (calculated as 2 × 10⁻⁴ / (2 × 10⁻⁴ + 10⁻³) ≈ 0.7)

## Why Posterior Doesn't Scale Linearly
- **Reason**: Posterior depends on both prior and likelihood. When prior increases, the denominator (total probability) also changes, limiting the posterior increase.
- **Example**: If prior increases by 10×, posterior increases by 4× (not 10×) because the evidence (alarm) is rare.
