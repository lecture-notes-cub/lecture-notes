# Marginalization in Probability Theory

## Key Definition
The marginal distribution of a random variable $x$ is obtained by integrating out (or summing over) other variables in the joint distribution. For example, the marginal distribution $P(x)$ can be derived from the conditional distribution $P(x|y)$ as:

$$P(x) = \int P(x|y) P(y) \, dy$$

This is equivalent to taking the expectation of $P(x|y)$ with respect to $y$.

## Physical Interpretation
The marginal distribution represents the average of conditional distributions over all possible values of $y$. As the speaker states: "we take all different conditional distributions on $x$ given different $y$'s, we average them out with respect to $y$ and we obtain unconditional distribution on $x$".

## Example
For two random variables $x$ and $y$, marginalizing $x$ from the joint distribution $P(x,y)$ gives $P(x)$ by integrating over $y$.
