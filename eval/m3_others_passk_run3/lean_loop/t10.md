# Bayesian Parameter Estimation vs. Point Estimates

## Key Definitions

- **Bayesian Inference**: A statistical approach that updates prior beliefs about a parameter (theta) using observed data to form a posterior distribution.
  - *Citation*: [00:32:13–00:32:53]

- **Prior Distribution**: A probability distribution representing initial knowledge or beliefs about the parameter theta before observing data.
  - *Citation*: [00:32:53–00:33:37]

- **Posterior Distribution**: The updated distribution of theta after incorporating observed data via Bayes' theorem.
  - *Citation*: [00:33:37–00:34:13]

## Core Reasoning

- Bayesian inference starts with a prior distribution over theta, then applies Bayes' theorem to compute the posterior distribution:
  
  $$
  P(\theta | X_1, X_n) \propto P(X_1, X_n | \theta) \cdot P(\theta)
  $$

  *Citation*: [00:33:37–00:34:13]

- The posterior distribution is proportional to the likelihood (product of observations) times the prior.

- Unlike maximum likelihood estimation (a point estimate), Bayesian inference yields a full distribution of possible values for theta.
  - *Citation*: [00:34:14–00:34:56]

## Contrast with Point Estimates

- **Frequentist Approach**: Uses maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) to find a single point estimate of theta.
- **Bayesian Approach**: Provides a posterior distribution, capturing uncertainty in theta.

- *Key Difference*: Frequentist methods give a point estimate (e.g., MLE), while Bayesian methods give a distribution (posterior) that reflects uncertainty.
