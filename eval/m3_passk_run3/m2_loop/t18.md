# Bayesian Reasoning Example: Radio Evidence and Model Misspecification

## Context
- The lecturer describes a scenario where they received an SMS from an alarm system indicating a thief in their house, and a radio message announcing an earthquake.
- They used Bayesian inference to compute the probability that there was actually a thief in their house given the radio announcement of an earthquake.

## Key Calculations
- The lecturer computed the posterior probability of a thief given the earthquake announcement (via radio) and alarm system.
- The calculation resulted in approximately **2%** probability that there was a thief in their house.
- This low posterior probability was based on the assumption that earthquakes and thieves occur independently.

## Model Misspecification
- The lecturer realized their model assumed independence between earthquakes and thieves.
- In reality, there is a dependency: thieves listen to the radio and may increase their activity after an earthquake announcement.
- This dependency causes the posterior probability of a thief to be higher when there's an earthquake announcement.

## Real-World Consequence
- The lecturer's model was too optimistic (predicted low probability of thief), but the model was correct in its predictions.
- The lecturer's apartment was later robbed, demonstrating the model misspecification.

## Why This Matters
- The example illustrates how Bayesian reasoning can extend classical Boolean logic.
- It shows the importance of correctly modeling dependencies in real-world scenarios.
