# Variance Interpretation: Observing Another Variable

## Key Definitions
- **Unconditional Variance of X** (Var(X)): Total variability of X without considering Y. [00:05:31–00:06:07]
- **Conditional Variance of X given Y** (Var(X|Y)): Variability of X when Y is known (depends on Y's value). [00:05:31–00:06:07]

## Core Reasoning
- The unconditional variance of X is **always ≥** the conditional variance of X given Y.
  - *Why?* Because observing Y provides information that reduces uncertainty about X. [00:05:31–00:06:07]
- This inequality holds due to the relationship: 
  $$\text{Var}(X) \geq \mathbb{E}[\text{Var}(X|Y)]$$
  (The unconditional variance is at least the expected conditional variance over Y.) [00:05:31–00:06:07]

## Example
- If X is the height of students and Y is their grade, observing Y (grade) can reduce the variance of X (height) by filtering out students with similar grades. [00:06:07–00:06:41]
