# Large-Data Limit and Practical Advantages of Bayesian Methods

## Large-Data Limit

In the limit where the number of observations $n$ is much larger than the number of parameters $d$ (i.e., $n \gg d$), the posterior distribution in Bayesian inference converges to a delta function centered at the maximum likelihood estimate (MLE). This means that for large datasets, Bayesian methods and classical maximum likelihood estimation become equivalent.

**Key evidence**: [00:53:22–00:54:26] "in the limit when n is much larger than d. So this means that we are trying to apply Bayesian inference in a situation where we know that maximum likelihood estimation is theoretically justified and is the best possible estimation method. And it can be proven that under those constraints, our two posterior distribution converges to the delta function around maximum likelihood estimate."

## Practical Advantages of Bayesian Framework

1. **Regularization of Maximum Likelihood Estimates**
   - Bayesian methods modify MLE when it's not optimal, providing regularization.
   - *Example*: Used in machine learning to handle overfitting.
   **Evidence**: [00:55:14–00:55:50] "it allows us to regularize the maximum likelihood estimates. In other words, it modifies maximum likelihood estimates for the situations when maximum likelihood estimation is not optimal."

2. **Building Complex Models from Simple Ones**
   - Can combine information from different sources within one framework.
   - *Example*: The elephant example mentioned in the lecture.
   **Evidence**: [00:55:52–00:57:05] "So second is can build complex models from simple ones, just like we did in this example with the elephant."

3. **Better Uncertainty Estimation**
   - Bayesian methods provide more reliable uncertainty estimates for predictions.
   - *Example*: Critical for deep neural networks that are often overconfident.
   **Evidence**: [00:57:05–00:57:44] "So they provide us some predictions and they're pretty sure about them even if those predictions are wrong. So many applications, this is especially crucial when we need to estimate the degree of uncertainty in the predictions."

4. **Latent Variable Models**
   - Bayesian inference enables the construction of latent variable models.
   **Evidence**: [00:57:05–00:57:44] "And finally, based on Bayesian inference, we can build so-called latent variable models."

## Key Clarification

The lecture clarifies that there is no contradiction between classical statistics and Bayesian frameworks. In the large-data limit ($n \gg d$), Bayesian posterior distributions converge to the MLE, meaning classical statistics can be viewed as a limit case of Bayesian inference. For smaller datasets ($n \not\gg d$), Bayesian methods extend classical statistics to handle more complex scenarios.

**Evidence**: [00:53:22–00:54:26] "the answer is actually no. Here we consider, so this can be even proven, but here we consider posterior distribution conditioned on a large number of observations... classical statistics can regard it as a limit case of Bayesian framework, or to the contrary, when we say that Bayesian framework is an extension of classical statistics, to the cases where n is not much larger than d."
