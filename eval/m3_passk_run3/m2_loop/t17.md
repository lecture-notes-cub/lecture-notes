# Bayesian Alarm Example: Posterior Calculations and Prior Effects

## Key Definitions
- **Prior Probability (P(t=1))**: Initial probability of a burglar being present (before evidence)
- **Posterior Probability (P(t=1|E))**: Updated probability after observing evidence (e.g., alarm)
- **Burglary Prior**: The base rate of burglary in the student's district

## Original Calculation (70% Posterior)
- **Prior**: P(t=1) = 2 × 10⁻¹ = 0.2
- **Likelihood**: P(E|t=1) = 2 × 10⁻⁴
- **Denominator**: P(E) = 2 × 10⁻⁴ + 10⁻³ = 1.2 × 10⁻³
- **Posterior**: P(t=1|E) = (0.2 × 2 × 10⁻⁴) / (1.2 × 10⁻³) = 0.333... ≈ 70% [01:19:19–01:20:27]

## Effect of Increasing Burglary Prior by 10x
- **New Prior**: P(t=1) = 2 × 10⁻³ (10x increase from original)
- **New Posterior**: P(t=1|E) = (2 × 10⁻³ × 2 × 10⁻⁴) / (2 × 10⁻³ × 2 × 10⁻⁴ + 10⁻³) = 0.666... ≈ 67% [01:22:42–01:23:13]
- **Change in Posterior**: Increased by 4x (from 70% → 67%) instead of 10x

## Why Posterior Doesn't Scale Linearly with Prior
- **Key Insight**: Posterior probability is constrained by the maximum value of 1. When the prior increases by 10x, the posterior only increases by 4x because the denominator (total probability) also scales with the prior.
- **Mathematical Reason**: The posterior is a ratio of the prior times likelihood to the total probability. When the prior increases, the denominator increases proportionally, limiting the posterior growth.
